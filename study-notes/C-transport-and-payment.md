# C. Transportation and payment of international business transactions

**Episodes 11–12** · Syllabus C1(a)–(e).

## Bills of lading (C1a)

A **bill of lading** is issued by the carrier, or the master or an agent, to the
shipper. It does three jobs:

1. **Receipt** for the goods: quantity and apparent order and condition, and
   usually that they are **shipped on board** a named vessel. A **clean** bill
   notes no defects. A **claused** (foul) bill records damage, and banks usually
   reject those.
2. **Evidence of the contract of carriage** between the shipper and the
   carrier.
3. **Document of title**: transferring a negotiable bill transfers the right to
   possession of the goods.

It also shows **when** the goods reached the carrier, which often marks when
risk passes (see area B, Art 67).

| Type | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Negotiable** ("to order" or bearer) | Transferred by endorsement and delivery. The holder can claim the goods |
| **Non-negotiable** (straight) | Names the consignee. The carrier delivers only to that person |
| **Inland** | Overland carriage to the port of departure |
| **Ocean** | Port to port |
| **Through** | Combines inland and ocean carriage, from the factory to the foreign port |
| **Air waybill** | Carriage by air. Always **non-negotiable**, so not a document of title |

International carriage regimes include the Hague-Visby Rules, the **Hamburg
Rules** (UNCITRAL, 1978) and the Rotterdam Rules (2008, not yet in force).

## Bank transfers (C1b)

A cross-border credit transfer is a **chain of payment orders**:

- the **originator** instructs the **originator's bank**
- that bank sends a payment order, usually by **SWIFT** messaging, often through
  an **intermediary** or correspondent bank
- the order reaches the **beneficiary's bank**, which credits the
  **beneficiary**

*Local lens:* the **East African Payment System (EAPS)** links East African
central banks. The **Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS)**
settles payments between African countries in local currencies.

## UNCITRAL Model Law on International Credit Transfers, 1992 (C1c)

- **Scope:** credit transfers where any **sending bank and its receiving bank
  are in different states**.
- **The sender's obligation:** to pay the receiving bank when that bank
  **accepts** the payment order.
- **The receiving bank's obligation:** once it accepts, to **execute** the
  order by issuing its own order, normally on the **day received**. If it
  rejects an order, it must give notice.
- **Money-back guarantee:** if the transfer is not **completed**, the
  originator's bank must **refund** the originator, **with interest**, and each
  receiving bank refunds its sender.
- **Delay:** a bank that delays must pay **interest**.
- **Completion:** the transfer is complete when the **beneficiary's bank
  accepts** the payment order for the beneficiary. The originator's debt to the
  beneficiary is then discharged, to that extent.

## UN Convention on International Bills of Exchange and International Promissory Notes, 1988 (C1d)

**A bill of exchange** is an **unconditional order in writing**, addressed by
one person (the **drawer**) to another (the **drawee**), **signed** by the
drawer. It requires the drawee to pay **on demand or at a fixed or determinable
future time** a **sum certain in money**, to or to the order of a specified
person (the **payee**) or to bearer. A **promissory note** is a *promise* by the
maker to pay.

**When is it "international" under the Convention?**

- It must be headed, and contain in its text, the words "International bill of
  exchange (UNCITRAL Convention)".
- It must specify **at least two** of five places: where it is drawn, next to
  the drawer's signature, next to the drawee's name, next to the payee's name,
  and the place of payment.
- **At least two** of those places must be in **different states**.
- *Status:* adopted in 1988 but **not in force**. It needs 10 states and has 5
  (Gabon, Guinea, Honduras, Liberia, Mexico). The exam still tests its rules.

**The parties:**

| Party | Who |
|---|---|
| Drawer | Draws the bill, usually the debtor's creditor (e.g. the exporter) |
| Drawee → **acceptor** | Signs "accepted" and becomes **primarily liable** |
| Payee → **indorser** | Signs the bill on to its own creditor |
| Indorsee / **holder** | Receives it. A bill endorsed **in blank** becomes a **bearer** bill and passes by delivery |
| **Guarantor** | Guarantees payment, presumed to be for the acceptor |
| **Protected holder** | Took a complete bill in good faith, without notice of defences. Takes free of most defences |

**Dishonour:** if the acceptor refuses payment (or the drawee refuses to accept),
the holder has **recourse** against **all prior signatories**: the drawer,
indorsers and guarantor. They are **jointly and severally** liable, and whoever
pays can claim back up the chain.

## Letters of credit and letters of comfort (C1e)

**A documentary letter of credit** gives the seller prompt payment and the buyer
credit, without either having to trust the other.

1. The buyer (**applicant**) asks its bank (**issuing bank**) to open a credit
   in favour of the seller (**beneficiary**).
2. The issuing bank undertakes to pay against **stated documents**, and asks a
   bank in the seller's country (**advising bank**) to notify the seller.
3. The seller ships the goods and presents the documents (bill of lading,
   invoice, insurance and so on).
4. The advising or nominated bank checks the documents, **pays the seller**, and
   forwards the documents.
5. The issuing bank checks them, reimburses the advising bank, debits the buyer
   and **releases the documents**, so the buyer can collect the goods.

**Principles (ICC UCP 600):**

- **Autonomy:** the credit is separate from the sale contract. Banks deal in
  **documents, not goods**.
- **Strict compliance:** the documents must match the credit on their face.
- **Irrevocable:** under UCP 600 every credit is irrevocable. It cannot be
  amended or cancelled without the beneficiary's consent. (Older texts mention
  revocable credits.)
- **Confirmed** credit: a second bank, usually the advising bank, adds its own
  undertaking to pay. **Unconfirmed**: the seller relies on the issuing bank
  alone.
- **Fraud exception:** a bank need not pay against documents it knows are
  fraudulent (*United City Merchants v Royal Bank of Canada*, 1983).

**A letter of comfort** is a letter from a parent company about its subsidiary,
for example "our policy is to ensure it meets its liabilities". It is usually a
statement of **present policy**, not a binding promise. In *Kleinwort Benson v
Malaysia Mining Corp* (1989) the lender could not recover from the parent. If a
lender needs the parent to be liable, it should take a **guarantee**.

## Exam traps

- An **air waybill** is never negotiable.
- The credit transfer is **complete on acceptance by the beneficiary's bank**,
  not when the beneficiary withdraws the cash.
- **Confirmed** means a second bank promises to pay. **Irrevocable** means it
  cannot be changed without consent.
- A bill **endorsed in blank** becomes a bearer bill.
