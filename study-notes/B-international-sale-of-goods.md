# B. International business transactions

**Episodes 5–10** · Syllabus B1 (the CISG and ICC Incoterms), B2 (obligations
of seller and buyer, and provisions common to both).

The **UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods** (CISG,
Vienna 1980) is in force in **about 97 states**. Notable states that have
**not** joined include the UK, **Tanzania** and **Kenya**. Uganda, Germany,
China and the USA have joined. Article numbers are given for revision. You
don't need to quote them in the exam, but they help you organise the rules.

## B1. Sphere, formation and Incoterms

### When the CISG applies (Arts 1–6)

- It covers contracts for the **sale of goods** between parties whose **places
  of business are in different states**, when either:
  - **(a)** both states are Contracting States, or
  - **(b)** the rules of private international law lead to the law of a
    Contracting State.
- **Nationality is irrelevant** (Art 1(3)). Place of business is what counts.
- **Excluded sales (Art 2):** consumer goods (personal, family or household
  use, unless the seller neither knew nor ought to have known); auctions;
  execution or other legal authority; stocks, shares, investment securities,
  negotiable instruments and money; ships, vessels, hovercraft and aircraft;
  electricity.
- **Goods to be manufactured** count as sales, unless the buyer supplies a
  substantial part of the materials. Contracts mainly for **labour or
  services** are excluded (Art 3).
- The CISG does **not** govern the **validity** of the contract, **property**
  (ownership) in the goods (Art 4), or liability for **death or personal
  injury** (Art 5).
- **Art 6:** the parties may exclude the Convention or vary its provisions.

*Local lens:* a Tanzanian buyer and a German seller. Route (a) fails, because
Tanzania is not a Contracting State. If the contract chooses German law, or the
conflict rules point to it, the CISG applies through route (b).

### General provisions (Arts 7–13)

- Interpret it with regard to its **international character**, the need for
  **uniformity**, and **good faith** in international trade. Fill gaps with the
  Convention's general principles, then with the applicable national law
  (Art 7).
- Statements are read by actual intent, if the other side knew it, or else as
  a **reasonable person** would understand them (Art 8).
- Agreed **usages** and established **practices** bind the parties (Art 9).
- **No writing is required.** The contract can be proved by any means,
  including witnesses (Art 11), unless a state has made a reservation.

### Forming the contract (Arts 14–24)

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Offer (Art 14) | A proposal to one or more specific persons, **sufficiently definite** (goods, and quantity and price fixed or determinable), showing intent to be bound. Proposals to the public are usually **invitations to offer** |
| Effect (Art 15) | An offer takes effect when it **reaches** the offeree. It can be **withdrawn** if the withdrawal arrives before or at the same time |
| Revocation (Art 16) | Possible until the offeree **dispatches** an acceptance. Not possible if the offer fixes a time or says it is irrevocable, or if the offeree reasonably relied on it |
| Rejection (Art 17) | Ends the offer when it reaches the offeror |
| Acceptance (Art 18) | A statement or conduct indicating assent. **Silence or inactivity alone is not acceptance.** Takes effect when it reaches the offeror, within the time fixed or a reasonable time. Oral offers must be accepted immediately. Acceptance by conduct is possible where practice or the offer allows |
| Counter-offer (Art 19) | A reply with additions or changes is a **rejection plus counter-offer**. If the changes are **immaterial**, it is an acceptance unless the offeror objects without undue delay. Changes to **price, payment, quality, quantity, place and time of delivery, liability, or dispute settlement** are always material |
| Time and late acceptance (Arts 20–21) | The acceptance period runs from dispatch or the date shown. A late acceptance is effective if the offeror promptly confirms it |
| Withdrawal of acceptance (Art 22) | Possible if the withdrawal reaches the offeror first or at the same time |
| Conclusion (Art 23) | The contract is concluded **when the acceptance becomes effective** |

### Incoterms® 2020 (ICC)

Incoterms say **who arranges and pays for carriage**, **who clears export and
import**, and **where risk passes**. They do **not** decide when **ownership**
passes, payment terms, or remedies for breach. They apply only if the contract
incorporates them, for example "CIP Dar es Salaam Incoterms® 2020".

| Rule | Family | Risk passes | Seller pays carriage to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EXW** Ex Works | Any mode | At the seller's premises, goods at the buyer's disposal | — | The buyer loads and clears export. Minimum obligation for the seller |
| **FCA** Free Carrier | Any mode | On delivery to the buyer's carrier | — | The seller clears export |
| **CPT** Carriage Paid To | Any mode | On delivery to the **first carrier** | The named destination | Two critical points |
| **CIP** Carriage and Insurance Paid To | Any mode | On delivery to the first carrier | The named destination | **All-risks cover (ICC A), 110%** |
| **DAP** Delivered at Place | Any mode | On arrival, **ready for unloading** | The named place | The buyer unloads and clears import |
| **DPU** Delivered at Place Unloaded | Any mode | **Once unloaded** | The named place | The only rule where the seller unloads |
| **DDP** Delivered Duty Paid | Any mode | On arrival, ready for unloading | The named place | The seller also **clears import and pays duties**. Maximum obligation |
| **FAS** Free Alongside Ship | Sea | **Alongside** the vessel at the port of shipment | — | |
| **FOB** Free On Board | Sea | **On board** the vessel | — | The buyer books the ship |
| **CFR** Cost and Freight | Sea | On board at the port of shipment | The destination port | Two critical points |
| **CIF** Cost, Insurance and Freight | Sea | On board at the port of shipment | The destination port | **Minimum cover (ICC C), 110%** |

Traps:

- With **C terms**, the seller pays further than it bears the risk.
- For **containers**, prefer FCA, CPT or CIP over FOB, CFR or CIF.
- The 2010 and 2020 rules no longer use the "ship's rail". Risk passes **on
  board**.

## B2. Obligations of seller and buyer

### Seller's obligations (Arts 30–44)

- **Deliver** the goods, **hand over documents**, and **transfer the property**
  in the goods (Art 30).
- **Place of delivery (Art 31)**, if none is agreed:
  - if carriage is involved, hand the goods to the **first carrier**
  - if the goods are specific or come from a known stock or place, put them **at
    the buyer's disposal** at that place
  - otherwise, at the **seller's place of business**
- Identify the consignment, arrange appropriate carriage, and give insurance
  information (Art 32).
- **Time (Art 33):** on the date fixed, within the agreed period, or a
  reasonable time.
- **Documents (Art 34):** as agreed. Defects can be cured before the due time.
- **Conformity (Art 35):** quantity, quality, description and packaging as the
  contract requires. Unless otherwise agreed, the goods must also be:
  - fit for **ordinary purposes**
  - fit for any **particular purpose** made known (unless the buyer did not rely
    on the seller)
  - like any **sample or model**
  - **packaged** in the usual or an adequate way

  The seller is not liable if the buyer **knew** of the defect when contracting.
- **Time of defect (Art 36):** the seller is liable for defects existing when
  **risk passes**, even if they appear later, and for later defects caused by
  its breach, including breach of a guarantee.
- **Early delivery (Art 37):** the seller may cure a shortfall or defect up to
  the delivery date, if that causes no unreasonable inconvenience or expense.
  The buyer keeps any damages claim.
- **The buyer's homework (Arts 38–40):** examine the goods **as soon as
  practicable**. This can be deferred if the goods are redirected. Give
  **notice** specifying the defect within a **reasonable time**, and **at the
  latest within 2 years** of delivery unless a longer guarantee applies. The
  seller can't rely on late notice if it **knew** of the defect and kept quiet.
- **Third-party rights (Arts 41–43):** goods must be free of third-party rights
  or claims unless the buyer agreed. For **IP**, the seller is liable only for
  rights it knew of or could not have been unaware of, under the law of the
  resale or use state (if contemplated) or else the buyer's state. It is not
  liable if the buyer knew, or if the claim arises from the buyer's own designs.
  The buyer must notify claims within a reasonable time.

### Buyer's remedies for the seller's breach (Arts 45–52)

1. **Require performance** (Art 46). **Substitute goods only for a fundamental
   breach**, repair if reasonable. A court need not order specific performance
   if its own law would not (Art 28).
2. **Fix an additional period** of reasonable length (Art 47).
3. **Avoid** the contract, by notice, for a **fundamental breach** or for
   non-delivery within the additional period (Art 49).
4. **Reduce the price** in proportion to the non-conformity (Art 50). Not
   possible if the seller cures.
5. **Damages**, in addition to any other remedy (Art 45).

The **seller may cure** even after the delivery date, without unreasonable
delay or inconvenience (Art 48). Partial delivery: remedies apply to the missing
part (Art 51). **Excess quantity:** the buyer may refuse the excess, or keep it
and pay the contract rate (Art 52).

**Fundamental breach (Art 25):** detriment that **substantially deprives** the
other party of what it was entitled to expect, unless the result was not
foreseeable.

### Buyer's obligations (Arts 53–60)

- **Pay the price** and **take delivery** (Art 53). Payment includes the steps
  needed to enable it, such as opening a letter of credit (Art 54).
- **No price fixed:** the price generally charged at the time of contracting
  (Art 55). **By weight:** use the net weight (Art 56).
- **Place:** the seller's place of business, or where goods or documents are
  handed over (Art 57).
- **Time:** when goods or documents are at the buyer's disposal. The seller may
  make payment a condition of handing over. The buyer may **examine before
  paying**, unless that is inconsistent with the agreed procedure (Art 58).
- Pay on the due date **without any request** (Art 59).
- **Taking delivery (Art 60):** do everything reasonably expected to enable
  delivery, such as nominating a ship under FOB, and take over the goods.

### Seller's remedies for the buyer's breach (Arts 61–65)

Require payment, delivery or performance (Art 62); fix an **additional period**
(Art 63); **avoid** for fundamental breach or failure within the period
(Art 64), with limits once the buyer has paid; make the **specification** if the
buyer fails to (Art 65); and **damages**.

**Retention of title:** in *Aluminium Industrie Vaassen v Romalpa* (1976) the
seller kept ownership until payment and recovered unsold goods and resale
proceeds. The CISG does not govern ownership (Art 4), so whether such a clause
works depends on national law.

### Provisions common to both (Arts 71–88)

- **Anticipatory breach (Arts 71–72):** if it becomes apparent the other party
  will not perform a substantial part, because of a serious deficiency in its
  ability, its **creditworthiness**, or its conduct, a party may **suspend**
  performance. It must **notify** at once, and continue if **adequate
  assurance** is given. A seller may **stop goods in transit** even if the buyer
  holds the documents. If a fundamental breach is **clear**, the party may
  **avoid in advance**, giving reasonable notice to allow assurance.
- **Instalment contracts (Art 73):** avoid **that instalment** if it is a
  fundamental breach. Avoid **future instalments** if there are good grounds to
  expect fundamental breach. Avoid **past ones too** if they are
  interdependent.
- **Damages (Arts 74–77):** the loss, including **lost profit**, limited to
  what was **foreseeable** at the time of contracting. After avoidance:
  - **substitute transaction:** the price difference (Art 75)
  - **current market price:** the difference (Art 76)

  Duty to **mitigate** (Art 77).
- **Interest (Art 78):** on the price or any sum in arrears, without prejudice
  to damages. The rate is not specified.
- **Exemption (Arts 79–80):** no liability in damages if the failure was due to
  an **impediment beyond control**, not reasonably foreseeable, and unavoidable.
  - **Notice** must be given.
  - If a **third party** caused it, both must qualify.
  - It lasts only while the impediment lasts.
  - It **blocks damages only**: avoidance, price reduction and interest remain.
- **Effects of avoidance (Arts 81–84):**
  - both parties are released, but **damages and dispute clauses survive**
  - **mutual restitution**
  - a seller refunding the price pays **interest**, and a buyer returning goods
    accounts for **benefits**
  - a buyer who cannot return the goods in substantially the same condition
    generally loses the right to avoid or demand substitutes (with exceptions)
- **Preservation (Arts 85–88):**
  - the **seller** preserves the goods if the buyer delays taking delivery, or
    fails to pay where payment and delivery are concurrent
  - the **buyer** preserves goods it means to reject
  - goods may be **deposited** in a third party's warehouse at the other's
    expense
  - goods may be **sold** after unreasonable delay, with notice
  - **perishable** goods must be sold, keeping costs and accounting for the
    balance

### Passing of risk (Arts 66–70)

- **Art 66:** loss after risk passes does not discharge the buyer from paying,
  unless the seller caused it.
- **Art 67 (carriage):** risk passes on handing the goods to the **first
  carrier**, or to the carrier at a particular place if one is specified.
  Keeping the documents does not affect this. The goods must be **clearly
  identified** to the contract.
- **Art 68 (goods sold in transit):** risk passes **when the contract is made**,
  or from handover to the carrier if the circumstances show it. The seller bears
  loss it knew of, or ought to have known of, and did not disclose.
- **Art 69 (other cases):** risk passes when the buyer **takes over** the goods,
  or when they are placed at its disposal and it breaches by not taking them.
- **Art 70:** if the seller has committed a **fundamental breach**, the buyer's
  remedies survive the passing of risk.

## Exam traps

- **Nationality is irrelevant. Place of business is decisive.**
- Silence is never acceptance. A change of price is always material.
- Substitute goods need a **fundamental** breach.
- The **2-year** long stop runs from when the goods were handed over.
- **Art 79** exempts from **damages only**.
- Goods sold in transit: risk usually passes **at the contract**.

## Practice questions

1. A Kenyan seller and a Tanzanian buyer choose Ugandan law. Does the CISG
   apply? *(Yes. Uganda is a Contracting State, so the conflict rules lead to
   the law of a Contracting State: Art 1(1)(b).)*
2. Under FOB, who books the ship and when does risk pass? *(The buyer; on board.)*
3. The buyer received the goods 26 months ago. It notices a defect for the first
   time and there is no guarantee. Can it claim? *(No: Art 39(2), unless the
   seller knew and kept quiet.)*
