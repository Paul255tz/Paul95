# F. Management, administration and regulation of companies

**Episodes 21–24** · Syllabus F1 (directors), F2 (other officers), F3
(meetings and resolutions). UK Companies Act 2006, CDDA 1986 model.

## F1. Company directors

**Role and types (F1a):** directors manage the company's business, collectively
as a **board**, and act as its agents. A director is anyone **occupying the
position**, whatever they are called.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Executive | Works in the business, under a service contract |
| Non-executive (NED) | Independent judgement and monitoring of the executives. Central to corporate governance |
| De jure | Validly appointed |
| De facto | Acts as a director without a valid appointment, and is treated as one |
| Shadow (s251) | A person whose directions the board is **accustomed to act on**. Professional advice alone doesn't count |
| Alternate | Stands in for an absent director, if the articles allow |

**Appointment, vacation, removal and disqualification (F1b):**

- **Numbers:** a private company needs at least **1** director, a public company
  at least **2**. At least one must be a natural person, aged **16+**.
- **Appointment:**
  - the first directors are named in the registration documents
  - after that, under the articles: in the model articles, by **ordinary
    resolution** or by the **board**
  - in a plc, appointments at a general meeting are voted on **individually**
  - the registrar is notified within **14 days**
  - from November 2025, UK directors must **verify their identity**
- **Leaving office:** resignation, retirement, death, bankruptcy,
  disqualification, medical grounds under the articles, or removal.
- **Removal (s168):**
  - by **ordinary resolution** with **special notice** (28 days to the
    company), at a **meeting**, never by written resolution
  - the director may circulate **written representations** and **speak**
  - weighted voting can protect a director (*Bushell v Faith*, 1970)
  - a removed director may still claim **damages** for breach of a service
    contract (*Southern Foundries v Shirlaw*, 1940)
- **Disqualification (CDDA 1986):**

| Ground | Maximum ban |
|---|---|
| Indictable offence connected with a company (s2) | 15 years |
| Persistent default in filing returns, e.g. 3 in 5 years (s3) | 5 years |
| Fraud in a winding up, fraudulent trading (s4) | 15 years |
| **Unfit** director of an **insolvent** company (s6) | **Mandatory**, 2 to 15 years |
| After an investigation (s8) | 15 years |
| Wrongful trading (s10) | 15 years |

  Acting while disqualified is a **crime**, and brings **personal liability**
  for the company's debts (s15). Disqualification **undertakings** are an
  alternative to a court order.

**Powers to bind the company (F1c):**

- Management power belongs to the **board collectively** (model article 3).
  Members can direct the board by **special resolution** (article 4).
- The board can **delegate** to committees or a **managing director**, who has
  authority for usual management contracts.
- An individual director has **no** authority alone unless it has been
  delegated, or the company has created **apparent authority**
  (*Freeman & Lockyer*; *Hely-Hutchinson v Brayhead*).
- **Outsiders are protected:**
  - for a third party in **good faith**, the directors' power is free of
    constitutional limits (s40)
  - under the **indoor management rule** (*Royal British Bank v Turquand*,
    1856), an outsider may assume internal procedures were followed

**Directors' duties (F1d, CA 2006 ss171–177):**

1. Act **within powers**, and for **proper purposes** (*Howard Smith v Ampol*,
   1974; *Hogg v Cramphorn*, 1967).
2. **Promote the success of the company** for the members as a whole, having
   regard to:
   - long-term consequences
   - employees
   - suppliers and customers
   - the community and environment
   - a reputation for high standards
   - fairness between members

   Near insolvency, creditors' interests must be considered (*BTI v Sequana*,
   2022).
3. Exercise **independent judgement**.
4. **Reasonable care, skill and diligence**, a dual test:
   - an **objective** minimum: what is reasonably expected of anyone in that
     role
   - plus the director's **own** knowledge and experience

   See *Re D'Jan of London* (1993) and *Dorchester Finance v Stebbing* (1989).
5. **Avoid conflicts of interest**, especially exploiting company property,
   information or opportunities (*Regal (Hastings) v Gulliver*, 1942; *IDC v
   Cooley*, 1972). Independent directors can authorise a conflict.
6. Do not accept **benefits from third parties**.
7. **Declare interests** in proposed transactions with the company.

*Local lens:* Kenya's Companies Act 2015 codifies essentially the same general
duties.

**Remedies for breach:** account for profits; compensation; rescission;
injunction. Members may **ratify** some breaches by ordinary resolution, without
the interested director's votes (s239). The court may **relieve** a director
who acted honestly and reasonably (s1157).

**Statutory controls (members' approval needed):**

- **Long-term service contracts** guaranteed for more than **2 years** (s188).
- **Substantial property transactions** with a director: a non-cash asset worth
  more than **£100,000**, or more than **10% of net assets** if that exceeds
  £5,000 (s190).
- **Loans** to directors, and guarantees or security for them (s197). For
  public companies and their associates, also **quasi-loans** and **credit
  transactions** (ss198–203).
  - Exceptions: expenditure on company business up to £50,000; defending
    proceedings; small loans and quasi-loans up to £10,000; credit transactions
    up to £15,000; money-lending companies in the ordinary course.
- **Payments for loss of office** (s215).

## F2. Other company officers

**Company secretary (F2a):**

- A **private** company does not need a secretary (s270) unless its articles
  require one. A **public** company must have one (s271).
- The secretary is appointed by the directors, who must ensure the person is
  **qualified** (s273). Examples: a member of a professional body such as
  **ACCA**, a lawyer, someone who was a plc secretary for 3 of the last 5 years,
  or someone the directors judge capable.
- **Duties** (set by the board, mainly administrative): statutory registers;
  filings (confirmation statement, accounts, charges); notices and minutes;
  governance advice; NED induction.
- **Authority:** apparent authority for **administrative contracts** only
  (*Panorama Developments v Fidelis Furnishing Fabrics*, 1971: hiring cars).
- *Local lens:* in Tanzania every company must have a secretary, and in Kenya
  larger private companies must too.

**Auditor (F2b):**

- **Requirement:** an audit is needed unless the company is exempt (e.g. small,
  dormant, or certain subsidiaries).
- **Appointment:**
  - the **directors** appoint the first auditor and fill casual vacancies
  - after that, the **members** appoint by ordinary resolution: in a plc at the
    accounts meeting; a private company's auditor is often **deemed
    reappointed**
  - the **Secretary of State** appoints if no one else does
- **Eligibility:** a member of a **recognised supervisory body** (e.g. ACCA) and
  **independent**. Not an officer or employee of the company, nor their partner
  or employee.
- **Rights:**
  - access to books and records **at all times**
  - **information and explanations** from officers and staff (misleading the
    auditor is an offence)
  - notice of, attendance at and the right to **speak** at general meetings
  - copies of **written resolutions**
- **Duties:**
  - report to the members on whether the accounts give a **true and fair view**
    and are properly prepared
  - report on the consistency of the directors' report, and for quoted
    companies the auditable part of the remuneration report
  - report **by exception**: inadequate records, accounts not agreeing with
    records, information not received
- **Duty of care:** owed to the **company and the members as a body**, not to
  individual investors (*Caparo Industries v Dickman*, 1990).
- **Removal:** by **ordinary resolution with special notice**, at a meeting.
  The auditor may make written representations and speak. The registrar is told
  within 14 days.
- **Resignation:** written notice to the registered office, plus a **statement
  of circumstances** connected with leaving. The auditor may **requisition a
  meeting** to explain.

## F3. Meetings and resolutions

**Types of meeting (F3a):**

- A **general meeting** is any meeting of members.
- A **public company** must hold an **AGM within 6 months** of its financial
  year end. Typical business: accounts, directors, auditor, dividend.
- A **private company** needs no AGM unless its articles require one.

**Resolutions (F3b):**

| Resolution | Majority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary** | **More than 50%** of votes cast | The default. Used to remove a director or auditor, with special notice |
| **Special** | **At least 75%** | Change the articles or name, reduce capital, voluntary winding up. Full text in the notice. File within 15 days |
| **Written** (private companies only) | Majority (ordinary) or 75% (special) **of all eligible votes** | **Cannot** remove a director or auditor. A copy goes to the auditor. Lapses after 28 days by default |

**Calling and conducting meetings (F3c):**

- **Who calls:**
  - the **directors**
  - members with **5%** of paid-up voting capital can require the directors to
    call one. The directors must call it within **21 days**, for a date within
    **28 days** of the notice. If they don't, the members can call it, at the
    company's expense
  - the **court** (e.g. deadlock)
  - a **resigning auditor**
  - for a plc, a serious loss of capital requires one
- **Notice:**
  - **14 clear days** for a general meeting; **21 days** for a plc AGM
  - **short notice** if members holding **90%** agree (private; up to 95% if the
    articles say so) or **95%** (public). A plc AGM needs **all** members
  - **special notice**: 28 days, to remove a director or auditor
  - the notice gives the date, time, place and general nature of the business,
    the text of any special resolution, and members' proxy rights
- **Members' resolutions and statements:** members with 5% of voting rights, or
  100 members holding an average of £100 paid up each, can require circulation
  of a statement (up to 1,000 words) or, in a plc, an AGM resolution.
- **Quorum:** **2** qualifying persons, or **1** in a single-member company,
  unless the articles say otherwise. If there is no quorum, the meeting is
  adjourned.
- **Proxies:** any member may appoint a proxy, who need not be a member, to
  attend, speak and vote (on a show of hands and on a poll) and to demand a
  poll. The deadline for proxy forms cannot be earlier than **48 hours** before
  the meeting. Corporate members appoint **representatives**.
- **Voting:**
  - a **show of hands** gives one vote per person present
  - a **poll** counts votes by shares
  - the articles cannot exclude a poll demanded by **5 members**, members with
    **10%** of voting rights, or members with **10%** of paid-up capital. The
    chair can also demand one
  - abstentions are not counted
- **Minutes** are kept for 10 years.

## Exam traps

- A director **cannot** be removed by written resolution, and nor can an
  auditor.
- Disqualification of an unfit director of an insolvent company is
  **mandatory**: at least 2 years.
- *Regal (Hastings)*: liability to account even when honest, and even when the
  company couldn't have taken the opportunity.
- *Caparo*: the duty is to the members **as a body**.
- A written resolution counts **all eligible** members, not just those who
  vote.
