# G. Insolvency law

**Episodes 25–26** · Syllabus G1(a)–(e). UK Insolvency Act 1986 (as amended)
model. *Local lens:* Tanzania has an Insolvency Act (Cap 25), and Kenya's
Insolvency Act 2015 includes administration.

## Voluntary liquidation (G1a)

| | Members' voluntary (MVL) | Creditors' voluntary (CVL) |
|---|---|---|
| Solvency | **Solvent** | **Insolvent** |
| Key document | Directors' **declaration of solvency**: after full inquiry, debts will be paid in full, with interest, within a stated period of **no more than 12 months**. Made within the **5 weeks** before the resolution and filed within 15 days. False declaration without reasonable grounds = offence | Directors' **statement of affairs** |
| Resolution | **Special resolution** (or ordinary, if the articles' fixed period has expired) | Special resolution |
| Liquidator chosen by | Members | **Creditors**, via a decision procedure (virtual meeting or deemed consent) within **14 days**. Their nominee prevails |
| Control | Members | Creditors, possibly with a liquidation committee |
| If insolvency appears later | Converts to a CVL | — |

## Compulsory liquidation (G1b)

**Petitioners:** the company, its directors, creditors, contributories, the
Secretary of State, the Official Receiver.

**Main grounds (IA s122):**

- the company has passed a **special resolution** to be wound up by the court
- a plc has had **no trading certificate** for more than 1 year after
  incorporation
- the company has not started business within a year, or has suspended it for
  a whole year
- it is **unable to pay its debts** (s123). For example, a creditor owed **more
  than £750** serves a **statutory demand** that is unpaid after **3 weeks**.
  Other tests: an unsatisfied execution; the cash-flow test; the balance-sheet
  test. The debt must not be genuinely disputed
- it is **just and equitable**:
  - deadlock (*Re Yenidje Tobacco*, 1916)
  - a member excluded from a quasi-partnership (*Ebrahimi v Westbourne
    Galleries*, 1973)
  - failure of the main object (*Re German Date Coffee*, 1882)

  This ground is only available if there is no more suitable remedy.

**Effects of the order:**

- The **Official Receiver** becomes liquidator, until an insolvency practitioner
  is appointed.
- Liquidation **commences at the presentation of the petition** (s129).
  Dispositions of property after that are **void** unless the court validates
  them (s127).
- Legal actions are **stayed**. The **directors' powers cease**. The order
  operates as notice dismissing the **employees**.
- The liquidator **realises** the assets and can challenge:
  - transactions at an **undervalue** (s238)
  - **preferences** (s239)
  - late **floating charges** (s245)
  - fraudulent and wrongful trading (ss213–214)
- After the final account, the company is **dissolved**.

## Order of distribution (G1c)

1. **Fixed charge holders**, out of the assets subject to their charge.
2. **Costs and expenses** of the liquidation.
3. **Ordinary preferential debts:** employees' arrears of wages (up to **4
   months** and **£800** each), accrued holiday pay, certain pension
   contributions.
4. **Secondary preferential debts:** **HMRC**, for VAT, PAYE income tax,
   employee NICs and CIS deductions (since 1 December 2020).
5. The **prescribed part**, ring-fenced from floating charge realisations for
   **unsecured creditors**: **50% of the first £10,000 + 20% of the rest**,
   capped at **£800,000** for charges created on or after 6 April 2020
   (previously £600,000).
6. **Floating charge holders.**
7. **Unsecured creditors**, including any excess wages, and sharing the
   prescribed part.
8. Statutory interest, then **deferred debts**, e.g. sums due to members as
   members, such as declared but unpaid dividends.
9. **Members**, according to their rights. Preference shareholders come first
   if their terms say so.

Each class is paid **in full** before the next. A class that can't be paid in
full shares **rateably**.

**Example:** floating charge assets of £100,000 give a prescribed part of
£5,000 + (20% × £90,000) = **£23,000** for the unsecured creditors.

## Administration (G1d–e)

**Purpose (Schedule B1, para 3), in strict order:**

1. **Rescue the company** as a going concern.
2. Achieve a **better result for creditors as a whole** than a winding up.
3. **Realise property** to distribute to secured or preferential creditors,
   without unnecessarily harming the creditors as a whole.

**Appointment:**

| Route | Who | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Court order** | The company, directors, or creditors (also a CVA supervisor or a liquidator) | The company is or is likely to become **unable to pay its debts**, and the order is **reasonably likely** to achieve the purpose |
| **Out of court** | A **qualifying floating charge holder** (charge over the whole, or substantially the whole, of the property) | The charge is enforceable. No need to prove insolvency |
| **Out of court** | The **company or its directors** | Must state the company is or is likely to become unable to pay its debts. Notice goes to the QFCH, who may appoint its own choice |

**Effects (the moratorium):**

- no winding-up resolution or order; pending petitions are dismissed or
  suspended
- no **enforcement of security**, and no **repossession** of goods (including
  hire purchase and retention-of-title goods), without the administrator's
  consent or the court's permission
- **legal proceedings** are frozen on the same basis
- any administrative receiver **vacates** office
- directors **stay**, but use management powers only with the administrator's
  **consent**
- company documents must state that it is in administration

**The administrator:**

- must be an **insolvency practitioner**; is an **officer of the court** and the
  **company's agent**
- must act in the interests of the **creditors as a whole**, as quickly and
  efficiently as reasonably practicable
- **Steps:**
  - notify the company, creditors and registrar (within 7 days), and publish
    the appointment
  - obtain a **statement of affairs**
  - send **proposals** to creditors within **8 weeks**, for their decision
  - then manage the business in line with the approved proposals
- **Powers:** do anything necessary to manage the business; **sell** assets;
  appoint or remove directors; call meetings; pay creditors (unsecured creditors
  only with the court's permission, unless via the prescribed part); challenge
  earlier transactions; bring wrongful trading claims.
- **End:** automatically after **1 year**, unless extended (by creditors, or by
  the court); earlier if the purpose is achieved; or by court order. It often
  exits into a CVL, a dissolution or a company voluntary arrangement.

**Advantages over liquidation:** the company may survive; the moratorium gives
breathing space; returns are often better; jobs and customers can be kept;
members may keep shares in a viable business; any creditor can apply.

## Exam traps

- An MVL needs a **declaration of solvency**. A CVL doesn't.
- The **£750** statutory demand, unpaid after **3 weeks**.
- Compulsory liquidation starts at the **petition**, not the order.
- **Preferential before floating.** The prescribed part comes out of floating
  charge assets.
- Administration's first objective is **rescue of the company**, not sale.
