# H. Corporate fraudulent and criminal behaviour

**Episodes 27–29** · Syllabus H1(a)–(f). UK model statutes: the Criminal Justice
Act 1993 (Part V), the UK Market Abuse Regulation and Financial Services Act
2012, the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, the Money Laundering Regulations 2017,
the Bribery Act 2010, the Criminal Finances Act 2017, the Economic Crime and
Corporate Transparency Act 2023, the Companies Act 2006 and the Insolvency Act
1986.

## H1(a) Insider dealing

**Offences (CJA 1993 s52), committed by an individual who has inside
information as an insider:**

1. **Dealing** in price-affected securities, on a regulated market or through
   a professional intermediary.
2. **Encouraging** another person to deal, whether or not they do.
3. **Disclosing** the information, other than in the proper performance of
   their employment, office or profession.

**Inside information (s56)** relates to particular securities or issuers, is
**specific or precise**, has **not been made public**, and would be likely to
have a **significant effect on the price** if it were.

**Insiders (s57):**

- **Primary insiders**: directors, employees or shareholders of an issuer, or
  people with access through their employment, office or profession (auditors,
  lawyers).
- **Secondary insiders** (tippees): people whose direct or indirect source is a
  primary insider.

**Defences (s53 and Schedule 1):** the accused did not expect a profit
attributable to the information being price-sensitive; believed on reasonable
grounds it had been widely disclosed; or would have acted the same way anyway.
There are special defences for market makers, market information and price
stabilisation.

**Penalties:** imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine on indictment. The
**contract is not void** (s63).

*Local lens:* Tanzania's **Capital Markets and Securities Act** (Dar es Salaam
Stock Exchange, regulated by the CMSA) and Kenya's **Capital Markets Act**
(Nairobi Securities Exchange, regulated by the CMA) also prohibit insider
dealing.

## H1(b) Market abuse

- **Civil regime (UK MAR, enforced by the FCA):** insider dealing, **unlawful
  disclosure** of inside information, and **market manipulation**.
  - Proof is on the **balance of probabilities**, and intent isn't always
    needed.
  - Sanctions: **unlimited fines**, public censure, injunctions, restitution,
    and prohibition orders.
  - Issuers must also disclose inside information promptly and keep insider
    lists.
- **Criminal offences (Financial Services Act 2012 ss89–91):** **misleading
  statements**, **misleading impressions**, and misconduct relating to
  **benchmarks**.
- **Market manipulation examples:** false or misleading signals; **wash
  trades**; **pump and dump**; spreading false rumours, including online;
  securing an artificial price (e.g. cornering supply).

## H1(c) Money laundering

**Definition and stages:** turning the proceeds of crime into assets that appear
legitimate.

1. **Placement**: the dirty money enters the system.
2. **Layering**: it moves through many transactions to hide its origin.
3. **Integration**: it comes back looking clean.

"Criminal property" covers the proceeds of **any** crime, including **tax
evasion**, theft and fraud.

**Offences (POCA 2002):**

| Offence | Section | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Concealing, disguising, converting, transferring or removing criminal property; entering an **arrangement**; **acquiring, using or possessing** it | ss327–329 | **14 years** |
| **Failure to disclose** (regulated sector: knowledge, suspicion or **reasonable grounds** to suspect) | s330 | 5 years |
| **Tipping off** (regulated sector) | s333A | 2 years |

The main defence is an **authorised disclosure** to the firm's **MLRO**, who
decides whether to file a suspicious activity report with the authorities.

**Policies, controls and procedures (MLR 2017):**

1. **Risk management practices:**
   - a **risk-based approach**
   - a written firm-wide risk assessment covering customers, countries or
     geographic areas, products or services, transactions, and delivery channels
2. **Internal controls:**
   - appoint a **nominated officer (MLRO)** and a responsible **board-level
     manager**
   - employee **screening** and **training**
   - independent **audit** of the system where appropriate
3. **Customer due diligence:**
   - identify and **verify** the customer
   - identify **beneficial owners** (generally more than 25% ownership or
     control)
   - understand the **purpose** of the relationship
   - **ongoing monitoring**
   - **Simplified** CDD is allowed for low risk (e.g. listed companies,
     regulated financial institutions).
   - **Enhanced** CDD is required for high risk: **politically exposed persons**
     and their family and close associates, high-risk third countries, complex
     or unusually large transactions, and other high-risk factors.
   - **If CDD can't be completed, don't act.**
4. **Reliance and record keeping:**
   - a firm may rely on another regulated firm's CDD, but **remains liable**
   - keep CDD and transaction records for **5 years** after the relationship
     ends
5. **Monitoring and managing compliance, and internal communication:**
   - Would every member of staff know who the MLRO is, and where the procedures
     are?
   - Can the firm **prove training** is done and current?
   - Would a random file show a risk assessment and complete CDD?
   - Does the pattern of internal reporting reveal teams that report unusually
     little?
   - A generic bought-in manual is not enough. Procedures must fit the firm's
     actual risks.

*Local lens:* both countries follow the **FATF** standards. Tanzania has the
**Anti-Money Laundering Act** (Cap 423) and its **Financial Intelligence Unit**.
Kenya has the **Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2009** and the
**Financial Reporting Centre**.

## H1(d) Bribery (Bribery Act 2010)

1. **Bribing** (s1): offering, promising or giving a financial or other
   advantage to induce or reward **improper performance** of a relevant
   function.
2. **Being bribed** (s2): requesting, agreeing to receive or accepting such an
   advantage.
3. **Bribing a foreign public official** (s6) to obtain or retain business.
4. **Failure of a commercial organisation to prevent bribery** (s7) by an
   **associated person** (an employee, agent or subsidiary performing services
   for it) intending to obtain or keep business or an advantage for it.
   Liability is strict.

**Defence to s7:** **adequate procedures**, based on six principles:

- proportionate procedures
- top-level commitment
- risk assessment
- due diligence
- communication, including training
- monitoring and review

**Penalties:** individuals up to **10 years** and/or an unlimited fine;
organisations an **unlimited fine**. **Facilitation payments are bribes.**
Reasonable, proportionate hospitality is allowed.

*Local lens:* Tanzania has the **Prevention and Combating of Corruption Act
2007**, enforced by the PCCB. Kenya has the **Bribery Act 2016** (which
requires private entities to have procedures to prevent bribery) and the
Anti-Corruption and Economic Crimes Act 2003, enforced by the EACC.

## H1(e) Criminal activity in companies; failure to prevent

**Failure to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion (Criminal Finances Act
2017, ss45–46):**

- A **relevant body** is a body corporate or a partnership.
- The offence has three stages:
  1. a taxpayer criminally evades tax
  2. an **associated person**, acting in that capacity, criminally
     **facilitates** the evasion
  3. the relevant body **failed to prevent** it
- It covers **UK** tax evasion, and **foreign** tax evasion where there is a UK
  connection.
- **Defence:** reasonable prevention procedures.
- **Penalty:** an unlimited fine.

**Failure to prevent fraud (ECCTA 2023 s199, in force 1 September 2025):**

- It applies to **large organisations** that meet at least two of: more than
  **250 employees**, more than **£36m turnover**, more than **£18m total
  assets**.
- An associated person commits a specified **fraud offence** (e.g. fraud by
  false representation, false accounting, fraudulent trading) intending to
  benefit the organisation or its clients.
- **Defence:** reasonable fraud prevention procedures.
- **Penalty:** an unlimited fine.
- ECCTA also attributes the economic crimes of **senior managers** to the
  organisation.

**Other offences in the life and liquidation of a company:**

- fraud (Fraud Act 2006) and false accounting
- fraud in anticipation of winding up, and misconduct in a winding up
- **phoenix** names: reusing a liquidated company's name (IA s216)
- acting while **disqualified**

## H1(f) Fraudulent and wrongful trading

| | Fraudulent trading | Wrongful trading |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | CA 2006 s993 (criminal); IA 1986 s213 (civil, in liquidation) | IA 1986 s214 (liquidation); s246ZB (administration) |
| Mental element | **Intent to defraud** creditors, or any fraudulent purpose. Actual dishonesty | **No dishonesty needed.** The director **knew or ought to have concluded** there was no reasonable prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation or administration |
| Who | Anyone **knowingly party** | **Directors**, including shadow and de facto directors |
| Consequence | Crime: up to **10 years** and/or a fine. Civil: a **contribution** to the assets | A civil **contribution**, plus possible **disqualification** |
| Defence | — | **Every step** to minimise potential loss to creditors |
| Standard | — | Objective plus subjective (like s174). *Re Produce Marketing Consortium* (1989) |

## Exam traps

- Insider dealing needs **specific, non-public, price-sensitive** information,
  and the contract is **not void**.
- Market abuse is mainly **civil**. Misleading statements and impressions are
  **criminal**.
- **Tipping off** is a separate offence. Report internally to the MLRO.
- The s7 Bribery Act defence is **adequate** procedures. The CFA and ECCTA
  defences are **reasonable** procedures.
- Failure to prevent fraud applies only to **large** organisations.
- **Fraudulent** trading needs intent. **Wrongful** trading doesn't.
