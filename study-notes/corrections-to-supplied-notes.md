# Corrections and updates to the supplied notes

The OpenTuition LW-GLO lecture notes (Sep 2026 – Jun 2027 edition) are a good
summary, but a few points are out of date or loosely worded. The episodes and
these study notes use the current position shown below. Where a point affects
an exam answer, follow the current rule and check it against ACCA's latest
examiner's reports and technical articles.

| Topic | Supplied notes say | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| Incoterms (CFR) | The seller pays costs until the goods "pass the ship's rail" | Since the 2010 rules, **risk passes when the goods are on board**. "Ship's rail" is no longer used |
| Incoterms: what they cover | They decide "at what moment ownership of the goods … pass[es]" | Incoterms do **not** deal with transfer of ownership. They cover delivery, costs, risk and clearance only (ICC) |
| Incoterms: CIP insurance | Not distinguished from CIF | Incoterms 2020: **CIP needs all-risks cover (Institute Cargo Clauses A)**, CIF only **minimum cover (Clauses C)**, both at 110% |
| Letters of credit | "Revocable" and "irrevocable" credits | Under **UCP 600** (2007), every credit is **irrevocable**. Revocable credits are now of historical interest only |
| Partnership size | "Normally a limit of 20 partners" | The 20-partner limit was **abolished in 2002**. There is no general maximum |
| Partnership dissolution | "Insanity of a partner" as a court ground | The ground is now **permanent incapacity** to perform the partnership agreement. Mental incapacity was removed from s35 in 2005 |
| Precedent | A ratio is not binding "if in conflict with European law" | After Brexit, retained or assimilated EU case law is treated differently. This point is not needed for LW-GLO |
| AGM timing (plc) | "Every calendar year… first within 18 months… then within 15 months" | **CA 2006 s336:** a public company must hold its AGM **within 6 months** of its financial year end |
| Directors | A corporate director change "scheduled to take effect by 2019" | Every company must have **at least one natural-person director**. ECCTA 2023 is phasing in further restrictions on corporate directors, and **identity verification** for directors and PSCs applies from **18 November 2025** |
| Directors' loans | Figures of £5,000, £10,000 and £100,000 for loans and quasi-loans | **CA 2006:** members must approve loans to directors (all companies) and quasi-loans and credit transactions (public companies). Exceptions: **£10,000** small loans and quasi-loans, **£15,000** credit transactions, **£50,000** business expenditure. Substantial property transactions: **> £100,000, or > 10% of net assets if more than £5,000** |
| Capital reduction | Always needs special resolution + power in the constitution + **court** consent | A **private company** may instead use a **special resolution supported by a solvency statement**, without the court (CA 2006 ss641–644). The court route remains for plcs |
| Treasury shares | Must be quoted on a stock exchange | Since 2013, **any** company may hold shares bought back out of distributable profits in treasury |
| Share premium uses | Includes writing off preliminary and formation expenses, and the premium on redemption | Under **CA 2006 s610**, the only uses are paying up **bonus shares** and writing off the **expenses of, and commission on, that issue** (plus reduction like capital) |
| Unclaimed dividends | "Statute barred after 6 years" | Under the **model articles**, unclaimed dividends may be **forfeited after 12 years**. A declared dividend is a debt from when it is due |
| Order of distribution | Liquidator's fees ranked equal first with fixed charges; HMRC not mentioned | Fixed charges are paid from charged assets, then **expenses**, then **preferential** debts, then **secondary preferential (HMRC)** since 1 Dec 2020, then the **prescribed part**, floating charges, unsecured creditors, deferred debts and members |
| Prescribed part | "Up to a maximum of £600,000" | **£800,000** for floating charges created on or after **6 April 2020** |
| Insider dealing | Information "less than 6 months old" | CJA 1993 has **no** 6-month rule. Inside information is specific or precise, not public, and likely to have a significant price effect |
| Tipping off | Maximum 5 years | **POCA s333A** (regulated sector) has a maximum of **2 years**. "Prejudicing an investigation" (s342) carries 5 years |
| Failure to prevent fraud | Not covered | A new corporate offence under **ECCTA 2023 s199**, in force **1 September 2025**, for large organisations |
| Notice of a special resolution | "14 days notice" | The notice period depends on the **meeting**, not the type of resolution: 14 clear days for a general meeting, 21 days for a plc AGM. A special resolution's exact text must appear in the notice |
