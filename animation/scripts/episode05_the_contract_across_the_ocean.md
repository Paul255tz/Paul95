**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Interpret it with regard to its international character, the need for uniformity, and good faith in international trade.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Gaps are filled by its general principles, and only then by the applicable national law.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The parties' statements are read by their intent, if the other side knew it, or else as a reasonable person would understand them.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Parties are bound by usages they agreed to, and by practices they have established between themselves.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a sales contract need not be in writing. It can be proved by any means, even by witnesses.

## Scene 5: Making an offer `3:10`

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus B1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** The life of an offer (Arts 14–17)
- Offer made — Definite: goods, quantity, price
- Reaches offeree — Now effective
- Withdrawal — Must arrive first or at the same time
- Revocation — Before acceptance is sent · not if irrevocable
- Rejection — Ends the offer
- Caption: Adverts and catalogues = invitations

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A contract needs an offer, and an acceptance.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An offer is a proposal addressed to one or more specific people. It must be sufficiently definite, naming the goods and fixing the quantity and the price, and it must show an intention to be bound.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Adverts, catalogues and price lists sent to the public are usually just invitations to make offers.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An offer takes effect when it reaches the offeree.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It can be withdrawn if the withdrawal arrives first, or at the same time.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It can be revoked until the offeree sends an acceptance. But not if the offer states a fixed time for acceptance, says it is irrevocable, or the offeree reasonably relied on it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a rejection ends the offer when it reaches the offeror.
- **HANS:** My email offered five machines at forty thousand euros each, open for fourteen days. So I can't revoke it during that time.

## Scene 6: Accepting, or countering? `4:12`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus B1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Acceptance vs counter-offer (Arts 18–19)
- **Acceptance:** Statement or conduct showing assent; Silence alone never counts; Effective when it reaches the offeror; Contract concluded at that moment; Immaterial changes: still acceptance unless offeror objects
- **Counter-offer:** Reply adds or changes terms; = rejection + new offer; Material: price, payment, quality, quantity, delivery, liability, disputes; Kito's €38,000 reply = counter-offer

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Acceptance is a statement, or conduct, showing assent. Silence or inactivity alone is never acceptance.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It takes effect when it reaches the offeror, within the time fixed, or a reasonable time. An oral offer must be accepted immediately, unless the circumstances show otherwise.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The contract is concluded at the moment the acceptance takes effect.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Now the trap. A reply that adds to, or changes, the offer is a rejection plus a counter-offer.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Unless the changes are immaterial. Then it is still an acceptance, unless the offeror objects without undue delay.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Changes to price, payment, quality, quantity, place and time of delivery, liability, or dispute settlement are always material.
- **KITO:** I replied: we accept, but at thirty-eight thousand euros.
- **NIA:** That changes the price. So it's a counter-offer, and there's no contract until Hans accepts it.
