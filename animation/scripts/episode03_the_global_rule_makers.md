## Scene 2: The UN and UNCITRAL `0:34`

*Teaching board · Syllabus A2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** UNCITRAL: how it harmonises
- Drafts conventions and model laws — CISG · Model Law on arbitration · Hamburg Rules
- Coordinates other organisations — Encourages cooperation, avoids duplication
- Promotes wider participation — More states joining existing conventions
- Consistent interpretation — Collects and publishes case law (CLOUT)
- Liaises with UNCTAD — UN Conference on Trade and Development
- Pop-up card **United Nations (1945)**: Peace and security · friendly relations · economic and social cooperation · human rights

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The United Nations, founded in 1945, aims to keep peace and security, build friendly relations between nations, and promote cooperation on economic and social problems and human rights.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** For trade law, its key body is UNCITRAL, the UN Commission on International Trade Law, set up in 1966.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** UNCITRAL prepares conventions, model laws and legal guides.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It coordinates the work of other organisations, so they do not duplicate each other.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It encourages more countries to join existing conventions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It promotes consistent interpretation, by collecting and publishing court decisions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And it works closely with UNCTAD, the UN trade and development body.
- **NIA:** So the Sales Convention, the Hamburg Rules on sea carriage, and the Model Law on arbitration all came from UNCITRAL.

## Scene 3: The World Trade Organization `1:36`

*Teaching board · Syllabus A2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** WTO: what it does
- Negotiates and administers agreements
- Reviews trade policies
- Helps developing countries
- Settles disputes between states
- Pop-up card **WTO · 1995 (from GATT 1947)**: Free trade in goods, services and IP: cut tariffs, quotas, import controls and red tape
- Pop-up card **Local lens: East Africa**: Tanzania and Kenya: WTO members since 1995 · EAC customs union with a common external tariff

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The WTO was formed in 1995, building on the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, the GATT, from 1947.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It promotes free trade in goods, services and intellectual property, by removing barriers: tariffs, quotas, import controls and red tape.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It is a forum for negotiating trade agreements, and it administers them once agreed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It reviews each member's trade policies.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It helps developing countries build their trade capacity.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And it settles trade disputes between member states.
- **KITO:** Are Tanzania and Kenya members?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Both have been members since the WTO began in 1995. Regionally, the East African Community customs union goes even further, with a common external tariff.

## Scene 4: The International Chamber of Commerce `2:34`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus A2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** ICC: rules made by business
- Nodes: ICC (1919); Trade, investment, fair competition; Fights commercial crime; Incoterms · UCP 600; Court of Arbitration (1923)
- Arrows: icc → trade; icc → crime; icc → rules; icc → court

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The ICC is the world business organisation, founded in 1919. It speaks for business, not governments.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It promotes trade, investment and open markets, and free and fair competition.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It fights commercial crime, such as fraud and counterfeiting.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And it writes rules that traders adopt in their contracts: Incoterms for delivery, and the UCP rules for letters of credit.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Its International Court of Arbitration, created in 1923, supervises ICC arbitrations around the world.
- **CAPTAIN CARGO:** Incoterms! I knew they'd come up!

## Scene 5: The OECD and UNIDROIT `3:19`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus A2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** OECD (Paris) · UNIDROIT (Rome, 1926)
- Nodes: OECD (Paris); MNE guidelines · anti-bribery · tax; General Assembly: decides; Governing Council: policy; Secretariat: day to day
- Arrows: oecd → oecd2; ga → gc (elects); gc → sec (supervises)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The OECD, based in Paris, brings together governments to share data and agree better economic policies.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Its standards reach far beyond its members: guidelines for multinational enterprises, an anti-bribery convention, and work on international tax.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** UNIDROIT, founded in Rome in 1926, is an independent intergovernmental body. It studies how to modernise and harmonise private law, especially trade law.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Its best-known work is the UNIDROIT Principles of International Commercial Contracts. And it has a three-tier structure.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The Secretariat runs it day to day.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The Governing Council sets policy, draws up the work programme, and supervises the Secretariat.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the General Assembly is the ultimate decision-maker. It approves the budget and the work programme, and elects the Council.

## Scene 6: Courts in international trade `4:21`

*Teaching board · Syllabus A2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Who resolves international disputes?
- International Court of Justice — The Hague · states only
- East African Court of Justice — Arusha · interprets the EAC Treaty
- WTO dispute settlement — Between member governments
- National courts and arbitration — ICC Court · NCIA Nairobi · TIAC Dar es Salaam

**Script**

- **NIA:** And when trading nations or companies fall out, which courts step in?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The International Court of Justice in The Hague hears disputes between states only. A company cannot sue there.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Regional courts interpret trade treaties. The East African Court of Justice sits right here in Arusha.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** WTO panels settle trade disputes between member governments.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But most commercial disputes go to national courts, or to arbitration, often supervised by bodies like the ICC Court, or the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration.
- **KITO:** Arbitration again. I think that's our next stop.
