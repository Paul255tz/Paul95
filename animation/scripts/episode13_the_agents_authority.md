## Scene 3: How agency is created `1:10`

*Teaching board · Syllabus D1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Creating agency
- Express agreement — Power of attorney: formal and written
- Implied agreement — From conduct or relationship
- Ratification — Adopted later, works backwards
- Estoppel — Held out as agent
- Necessity — Emergency, owner unreachable, good faith

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Agency can arise in five ways.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** By express agreement, written or oral. A power of attorney, giving wide authority, must be made formally in writing.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** By implied agreement, from the parties' conduct or relationship.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** By ratification, where the principal later adopts a contract made without authority. It works backwards, as if authorised all along.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** By estoppel, where the principal holds someone out as its agent, and cannot later deny it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And by necessity, in a genuine emergency, when the owner of goods cannot be contacted, and someone acts in good faith to protect them.
- **NIA:** Like a carrier selling perishable fruit before it rots, when the owner can't be reached.

## Scene 4: Ratification's limits `2:00`

*Case file · SafariTech office, Dar es Salaam · Syllabus D1(b)*

**On screen**

- **Kelner v Baxter** (England · 1866)
  - Rule: Principal must exist and have capacity when the contract is made
  - Facts: Promoters bought wine for a company not yet formed
  - Held: No ratification possible · promoters personally liable
  - Stamp: CANNOT RATIFY

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Ratification has strict conditions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The agent must have claimed to act for that principal. The principal must have existed, and had capacity, when the contract was made.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the principal must ratify the whole contract, within a reasonable time.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Kelner against Baxter, promoters bought wine for a hotel company that had not yet been formed. The company later tried to ratify.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It could not, because it did not exist when the contract was made. The promoters were personally liable to pay.
- **KITO:** So I'll register SafariTech Uganda before my agent signs anything in its name!

## Scene 5: Types of authority `2:44`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus D1(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Authority of the agent
- **Express:** What the principal actually said; Written or oral
- **Implied:** Incidental to the task; Usual for that position; Unless forbidden
- **Apparent:** Principal's representation; Made by the principal, not the agent; Third party relied on it; And acted on it

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An agent's authority comes in three kinds.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Express actual authority: what the principal actually said the agent may do.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Implied actual authority: what is incidental to that, or usual for an agent in that position, unless the principal has forbidden it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And apparent, or ostensible, authority: what the principal has led the third party to believe the agent may do, even if it was never actually given.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Apparent authority needs three things. A representation by the principal, not by the agent. Reliance on it by the third party. And the third party acting on it.
- **NIA:** So if Kito tells shops that the agent can sign deals of any size, SafariTech is bound, even if he privately capped her at ten thousand dollars.

## Scene 6: Apparent authority in action `3:37`

*Case file · Boardroom · Syllabus D1(c)*

**On screen**

- **Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park** (Court of Appeal, England · 1964)
  - Facts: Director acted as MD with the board's knowledge
  - Issue: He hired architects; company refused to pay
  - Held: Company bound by his apparent authority
  - Stamp: COMPANY LIABLE
- Caption: Hely-Hutchinson v Brayhead (1968): implied actual authority

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Freeman and Lockyer against Buckhurst Park Properties, a director acted as if he were the managing director, and the board knew and let him.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** He hired architects for the company. The company refused to pay, saying he had never been appointed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The court held the company liable. By allowing him to act as managing director, it represented that he had that authority.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Compare Hely-Hutchinson against Brayhead. A chairman acting as chief executive had implied actual authority, from the board's acceptance of his role.

## Scene 7: Who is liable? `4:18`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus D1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Liability of principal and agent
- **Principal liable:** Agent within actual authority; Or within apparent authority; Agent drops out of the contract
- **Agent personally liable:** Breach of warranty of authority (Collen v Wright); Principal undisclosed; Principal did not exist; Trade custom or contract form
- Pop-up card **Ending an agency**: Agreement · revocation · death · insanity · principal's bankruptcy. Notify third parties!

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** When an agent acts within actual or apparent authority, the principal is liable on the contract, and the agent usually cannot sue or be sued on it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But the agent can be personally liable in some cases.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If it acted without authority, it is liable for breach of warranty of authority, as in Collen against Wright.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If it hid the principal's existence, the third party may sue the agent, or the principal once discovered.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the principal did not exist, as with pre-incorporation contracts.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Or if trade custom, or the form of the contract, makes the agent liable.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And an agency ends by agreement, by revocation, or automatically on death, insanity, or the principal's bankruptcy. But tell third parties, or apparent authority may linger.
