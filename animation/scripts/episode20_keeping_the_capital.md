## Scene 2: The creditors' buffer

*Priority waterfall · Studio · Syllabus E3(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** The capital buffer
1. Share capital
2. Share premium account
3. Capital redemption reserve
4. Net unrealised profits — e.g. revaluation surplus
5. Other undistributable reserves
- Caption: Trevor v Whitworth (1887)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Trevor against Whitworth, the court said a company's capital must not be returned to members, except as the law allows.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The buffer is share capital plus the undistributable reserves.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Share capital itself.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The share premium account.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The capital redemption reserve, created when shares are bought back.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Unrealised profits, such as revaluation gains, to the extent they exceed unrealised losses.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And any other reserve the law or the articles say cannot be distributed.
- **NIA:** So only true, realised profits can ever go out as dividends.

## Scene 3: Reducing capital

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus E3(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Capital reduction
- **Solvency statement:** Private companies only; Special resolution; All directors: debts paid for 12 months; False statement = crime
- **Court route:** Any company; required for plc; Special resolution + court confirmation; Creditors may object; plc below £50,000: re-register private
- Pop-up card **Why reduce?**: Write off lost capital · cancel unpaid liability · return surplus capital

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A company can still reduce its capital, under strict control, and only if its articles don't forbid it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Typical reasons: capital lost through trading losses, cancelling unpaid liability on shares, or returning surplus capital to members.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A private company can pass a special resolution, backed by a solvency statement. Every director states the company can pay its debts for the next twelve months.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A false statement is a criminal offence.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Any company, and every public company, can instead pass a special resolution confirmed by the court. The court protects creditors, who may object.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And if a public company's capital drops below fifty thousand pounds, it must re-register as private, unless the court allows otherwise.

## Scene 4: Other safeguards

*Teaching board · Syllabus E3(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** More capital guards
- Buy-backs from distributable profits — Or a fresh issue · capital redemption reserve
- No financial assistance (plc) — To buy the company's own shares
- Serious loss of capital (plc) — Net assets ≤ half called-up capital: meeting in 28 days

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Capital maintenance has other guards too.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A company buying back or redeeming its own shares must generally use distributable profits, or a fresh share issue, and keep the buffer up through the capital redemption reserve.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company may not give financial assistance for someone to buy its own shares, such as lending them the money.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And if a public company's net assets fall to half or less of its called-up share capital, the directors must call a general meeting within twenty-eight days.
- **THE AUDITOR:** That last one is called a serious loss of capital.

## Scene 5: Profits available for dividends

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus E3(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Distributable profits
- **Private company:** Accumulated realised profits; less accumulated realised losses; Capital or revenue: no difference
- **Public company:** Same profits test, plus…; Net assets at least called-up capital + undistributable reserves; Unrealised losses count
- Pop-up card **Safari Solar Ltd**: $200,000 realised profit − $80,000 realised loss = $120,000 available

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Dividends can only be paid from profits available for the purpose.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** For any company, that means accumulated realised profits, less accumulated realised losses. Past years count, and there is no difference between capital and revenue profits.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company must also pass a net assets test. After the dividend, its net assets must not fall below its called-up share capital plus undistributable reserves.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** So unrealised losses, which a private company can ignore, can still block a public company's dividend.
- **NIA:** Safari Solar's realised profits are two hundred thousand, and last year's realised loss was eighty thousand. So we can pay up to one hundred and twenty thousand.

## Scene 6: Declaring and getting it wrong

*Teaching board · Syllabus E3(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Paying dividends
- Declared by members (ordinary resolution) — Never above the directors' recommendation
- Declared = a debt to members
- Unlawful? Knowing members repay
- Directors personally liable — Re Exchange Banking Co (Flitcroft's case, 1882)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Under the model articles, members declare a final dividend by ordinary resolution, but never more than the directors recommend. Directors may pay interim dividends.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Once declared, a dividend becomes a debt owed to the member.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If a dividend is unlawful, a member who knew, or had reasonable grounds to believe, it was unlawful must repay it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And directors who authorised it can be personally liable to repay the company, as in Flitcroft's case.
- **KITO:** Then we'll pay a modest, lawful dividend, and keep the bank happy.
