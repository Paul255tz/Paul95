- A business — Trade, occupation or profession
- Carried on — Continuing, not usually a one-off
- In common — For all the partners
- With a view of profit — Sharing profits = strong evidence

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Break the definition down.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Persons: at least two. They can be individuals, or companies.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A business: any trade, occupation or profession.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Carried on: it should be continuing, not a one-off deal, although a single venture can sometimes count.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In common: run for the benefit of all the partners.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** With a view of profit. Sharing profits is strong evidence of a partnership, but merely sharing gross takings is not.
- **NIA:** And in England, a partnership is not a separate legal person. The partners are the firm.

## Scene 3: The default rules `1:23`

*Teaching board · Syllabus D2(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Default rules (1890 Act)
- Equal shares of capital and profit
- 5% interest on advances, none on capital
- All manage · no salary
- Indemnity for firm liabilities
- Majority, but unanimity for new partner or business
- Books open · no expulsion · no secret profits

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Partners can agree almost anything. Where they haven't, the Act fills the gaps.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Capital and profits are shared equally, and losses too.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** No interest on capital, but five percent a year on loans a partner makes beyond agreed capital.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Every partner may take part in management, but no partner is entitled to a salary.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A partner is indemnified for liabilities properly incurred for the firm.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Ordinary matters are decided by majority. But changing the business, or admitting a new partner, needs everyone's consent.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The books are open to every partner. No expulsion unless the agreement allows it. And secret profits, or profits from a competing business, must be handed over to the firm.
- **KITO:** Fifty-fifty works for me. But no salary? Hmm.

## Scene 4: A partner's authority `2:18`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus D2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Partners as agents (ss 5–8, 14)
- Nodes: Kito orders lamps; Firm bound (and Nia); Supplier
- Arrows: k → s (usual business); s → f (can sue)
- Pop-up card **Not bound if…**: No authority AND the third party knew it, or didn't know he was a partner
- Caption: Private use of the firm's credit: not binding
- Caption: Held out as a partner? Liable as one

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Every partner is an agent of the firm, and of the other partners, for carrying on business of the kind the firm does, in the usual way.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** So the firm is bound, unless the partner had no authority, and the third party knew that, or did not know or believe he was a partner.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A partner using the firm's credit for private purposes does not bind the firm, unless the others authorised it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And anyone who lets themselves be held out as a partner is liable, like a partner, to those who gave credit on that basis.
- **NIA:** So if Kito orders lamps for Safari Solar, I'm bound too, even if we agreed only I would order.

## Scene 5: Liability for the firm's debts `3:03`

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus D2(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Who is liable, and when?
- Unlimited liability — Jointly for debts, jointly and severally for wrongs
- Incoming partner — Not liable for earlier debts
- Retiring partner — Liable for debts while a partner
- Give notice! — Customers told · Gazette notice
- Novation — Creditor agrees to release

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In an ordinary partnership, liability is unlimited. Partners are jointly liable for the firm's debts, and jointly and severally liable for its wrongs.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In practice, a creditor can pursue any partner for the whole debt, and that partner claims a contribution from the others.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A new partner is not liable for debts incurred before joining.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A retiring partner stays liable for debts incurred while a partner.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And for debts incurred after retiring, unless notice was given: actual notice to existing customers, and a public notice in the Gazette for everyone else.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A retiring partner can be released from old debts by novation, a new agreement with the creditor.

## Scene 6: Ending a partnership `3:53`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus D2(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Dissolution (ss 32–35)
- **Automatic:** Fixed term expires; Single venture completed; Notice (partnership at will); Death or bankruptcy of a partner; Business becomes illegal; Share charged: others may dissolve
- **By the court:** Permanent incapacity; Prejudicial conduct; Persistent breach; Business only at a loss; Just and equitable

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Unless agreed otherwise, a partnership is dissolved automatically when its fixed term ends, its single venture is complete, a partner gives notice in a partnership at will, or a partner dies or becomes bankrupt.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Also if the business becomes illegal. And if a partner's share is charged for a private debt, the others may choose to dissolve.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A court may order dissolution for a partner's permanent incapacity, conduct prejudicial to the business, persistent breach of the agreement, when the business can only run at a loss, or when it is just and equitable.
- **NIA:** So the first group happens automatically, but the second group needs a judge.

## Scene 7: Sharing out after dissolution `4:38`

3. Partners' capital
4. Surplus in profit-sharing ratio
- Animated: money pours down the tiers in order of priority
- Caption: Garner v Murray: deficiency shared by capital ratio

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** On dissolution, the partners have authority to wind up the firm. Then the assets are paid out in this order.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** First, outside creditors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Second, partners' loans and advances to the firm.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Third, each partner's capital.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Fourth, anything left is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Losses are met from profits, then capital, and then by the partners personally. If one partner cannot pay a capital deficiency, the others bear it in proportion to their capital, under Garner against Murray.

## Scene 8: Limited and LLP `5:20`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus D2(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** General · limited · LLP
- **General:** Unlimited liability; No separate personality (England); No formality needed
- **Limited (1907):** 1+ general, 1+ limited partner; Must register; Limited partner: no management; Can't withdraw capital
- **LLP (2000):** Separate legal person; Registered like a company; Members' liability limited; Files accounts; Wrongful / fraudulent trading apply

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Two variations limit liability.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A limited partnership, under the 1907 Act, needs at least one general partner with unlimited liability, and at least one limited partner. It must be registered.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The limited partner's liability is limited to its capital. But it must not take part in management, or it loses that protection, and it cannot withdraw its capital.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A limited liability partnership, under the 2000 Act, is different. It is a separate legal person, registered like a company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Members' liability is limited. It must file accounts, usually audited, and the rules on fraudulent and wrongful trading still apply.
- **KITO:** And Kenya has its own Partnerships Act, and a Limited Liability Partnerships Act, too!

## Scene 9: Partnerships around the world `6:14`

*Teaching board · Syllabus D2*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Partnerships abroad
- Société en nom collectif — France · like a general partnership
- Société en commandite simple — France · like a limited partnership
- Musharakah — Pool capital · share profit, loss, decisions
- Mudarabah — One funds, one manages · profit share
- Shirkah al-inan — Invest, share profits · no liability for others

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Other systems have their own versions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In France, a société en nom collectif works like a general partnership.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a société en commandite simple is like a limited partnership.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Islamic law recognises musharakah, where partners pool capital and share profits, losses and decisions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Mudarabah, where one partner provides capital and the other manages, sharing profit instead of paying interest.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And shirkah al-inan, where partners invest and share profits, but are not liable for each other's debts.
- **NIA:** Mudarabah sounds like a limited partnership, but without interest.
