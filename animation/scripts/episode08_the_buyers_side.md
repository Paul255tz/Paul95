## Scene 2: Paying the price `0:35`

*Teaching board · Syllabus B2(b)(i)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Payment rules (Arts 54–59)
- No price fixed? — Price generally charged at the time of contract
- Priced by weight? — Net weight
- Where? — Seller's place, or where goods / documents are handed over
- When? — When goods or documents are at the buyer's disposal
- Examine first — Unless the payment method rules it out
- No reminder needed — Pay on the due date
- Pop-up card **Art 54: steps to enable payment**: e.g. open a letter of credit, obtain a bank guarantee

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Paying includes the steps needed to make payment possible, like opening a letter of credit, or getting a bank guarantee.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If no price was fixed, it is the price generally charged for such goods when the contract was made.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the price depends on weight, use the net weight.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Place: the seller's place of business, or, if payment is against goods or documents, wherever they are handed over.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Time: when the seller places the goods or documents at the buyer's disposal. The seller can make payment a condition of handing them over.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The buyer may examine the goods before paying, unless the agreed payment method rules that out.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the buyer must pay on the due date, without any request from the seller.
- **AMARA:** That's why our bank asks importers to set up payment terms before the goods ship.

## Scene 3: Taking delivery `1:30`

*Animated diagram · Port of Dar es Salaam · Syllabus B2(b)(ii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Taking delivery (Art 60)
- Nodes: Do what is reasonably expected so seller can deliver; Take over the goods
- Arrows: a → b (then)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Taking delivery has two parts.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** First, doing everything that can reasonably be expected so the seller can deliver. For example, sending a ship under FOB, or giving specifications.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Second, actually taking over the goods.
- **CAPTAIN CARGO:** So if nobody collects me at the port, that's the buyer's breach!
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Exactly. And even a buyer who rejects goods must look after them. We'll see that duty in the next episode.

## Scene 4: The seller's remedies `2:01`

*Teaching board · Syllabus B2(b)(iii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Seller's remedies (Arts 61–65)
- Require payment or performance
- Fix an additional period
- Avoid the contract — Fundamental breach or deadline missed
- Make the missing specification
- Damages — Alongside other remedies
- Caption: Buyer has paid? Seller's right to avoid is limited

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the buyer breaches, the seller's toolkit mirrors the buyer's.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Require the buyer to pay, take delivery, or perform its other obligations, unless the seller has chosen an inconsistent remedy.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Fix an additional period of reasonable length for the buyer to perform.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Avoid the contract, for a fundamental breach, or if the buyer does not pay or take delivery within the additional period.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the buyer fails to give specifications, the seller may supply them itself.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And claim damages, alongside any other remedy.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** One limit: once the buyer has paid, the seller generally loses the right to avoid, unless it acts quickly about late performance or another breach.
- **HANS:** So I could fix a deadline, and then avoid the contract if SafariTech still doesn't pay.

## Scene 5: Retention of title `2:56`

*Case file · SafariTech office, Dar es Salaam · Syllabus B2(b)*

**On screen**

- **Aluminium Industrie Vaassen v Romalpa Aluminium** (Court of Appeal, England · 1976)
  - Facts: Foil sold with title kept until payment · buyer became insolvent
  - Held: Seller recovered unsold foil and resale proceeds held for it
  - CISG: Ownership is outside the Convention (Art 4): national law decides
  - Stamp: TITLE RETAINED

**Script**

- **HANS:** Next time, I'll keep ownership of my machines until I'm paid.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** That is a retention of title clause, often called a Romalpa clause, after a famous English case.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A Dutch supplier sold aluminium foil to an English company, with a clause keeping ownership until payment. The buyer became insolvent.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The court held the supplier could recover its unsold foil, and the proceeds of resales held for it, ahead of the other creditors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But remember: the CISG does not deal with ownership. Whether such a clause works depends on the national law that applies.
