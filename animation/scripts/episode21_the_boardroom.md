## Scene 2: Kinds of director

*Teaching board · Syllabus F1(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Types of director
- Executive — Works in the business
- Non-executive — Independent challenge
- De jure — Validly appointed
- De facto — Acts as one, no valid appointment
- Shadow — Board accustomed to obey them
- Alternate — Stands in, if articles allow

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A director is anyone occupying the position of director, whatever they're called.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Executive directors work in the business, usually under service contracts, like Kito and Nia.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Non-executive directors bring independent judgement, monitor the executives, and are central to good corporate governance.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A de jure director is validly appointed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A de facto director acts as a director without a valid appointment, and is treated as one.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A shadow director is someone whose instructions the directors are accustomed to follow. Professional advice alone doesn't count.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And an alternate director stands in for a director who can't attend, if the articles allow.
- **NIA:** So a hidden boss pulling the strings can still owe directors' duties.

## Scene 3: Appointment

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus F1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Becoming a director
- Eligibility — 1 (private) or 2 (plc) · a person, 16+
- Appointed — Ordinary resolution or by the board
- plc rules — Separate resolutions · often annual election
- Tell the registrar — 14 days · identity verified

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A private company needs at least one director, and a public company at least two. At least one must be a real person, aged at least sixteen.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The first directors are named in the registration documents. After that, the articles decide. Under the model articles, directors are appointed by ordinary resolution, or by the board.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In a public company, each appointment at a general meeting needs its own resolution, unless members unanimously agree otherwise. Many listed companies put every director up for re-election each year.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The registrar must be told within fourteen days. And in the UK, directors must now verify their identity with Companies House.
- **AMARA:** And under our loan agreement, the bank may nominate one director.

## Scene 4: Leaving, and being removed

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus F1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Removing a director
- Special notice — 28 days to the company
- Notice to members — Usually 21 days
- Director's say — Written reps · speak
- Ordinary resolution — At a meeting, never written
- Caption: Southern Foundries v Shirlaw (1940): damages for breach

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A director leaves office by resigning, by retiring, on death, on disqualification or bankruptcy, on medical grounds under the articles, or by removal.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Members can always remove a director by ordinary resolution, whatever the articles or any contract say.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Special notice of the resolution must reach the company at least twenty-eight days before the meeting.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The company tells the members, usually with twenty-one days' notice.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The director may circulate written representations, and speak at the meeting.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And it must be a meeting. A written resolution cannot remove a director.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A removed director may still claim compensation for breach of a service contract, as in Southern Foundries against Shirlaw.

## Scene 5: Weighted votes

*Case file · Boardroom · Syllabus F1(b)*

**On screen**

- **Bushell v Faith** (House of Lords · 1970)
  - Facts: Articles: director's shares get 3 votes each on his removal
  - Held: Valid: 300 votes beat 200
  - Point: Weighted voting can block removal
  - Stamp: WEIGHTED VOTES OK

**Script**

- **KITO:** So the shareholders could fire me anytime?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In principle. But look at Bushell against Faith.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Three siblings held a hundred shares each. The articles said that on a resolution to remove a director, that director's shares carried three votes each.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** When the other two tried to remove their brother, he had three hundred votes against their two hundred. The House of Lords upheld the clause.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** So in small private companies, weighted voting rights can protect a director from removal.

## Scene 6: Disqualification

*Teaching board · Syllabus F1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** CDDA 1986: grounds and bans
- Serious company offence — Up to 15 years
- Persistent filing defaults — 3 in 5 years · up to 5 years
- Fraud, fraudulent or wrongful trading — Up to 15 years
- Unfit director of insolvent company — Mandatory: 2 to 15 years
- Caption: Breach: a crime + personal liability for debts

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Under the Company Directors Disqualification Act, a court can ban unfit people from acting as directors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** After a conviction for a serious offence connected with a company: up to fifteen years.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** For persistent breaches of filing rules, three defaults in five years: up to five years.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** For fraud in a winding up, or fraudulent or wrongful trading: up to fifteen years.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And for unfit conduct as a director of an insolvent company, a court must disqualify, for at least two years and up to fifteen.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A disqualified person cannot act as a director, or take part in managing a company, without the court's permission. Breach is a crime, with personal liability for the company's debts.

## Scene 7: Who can bind the company?

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus F1(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Powers to bind the company
- Nodes: Members; Board (collective); Managing director; Individual director; Third party (good faith)
- Arrows: m → b (special resolution); b → md (delegates); b → d (only if delegated); b → t (binds)
- Caption: Turquand (1856): indoor management rule

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The articles give management power to the board, acting collectively. Members can only direct them by special resolution.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The board can delegate to committees, or to a managing director, who has authority to make the usual management contracts.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An individual director has no authority alone, unless it is delegated, or the company lets them appear to have it, as in Freeman and Lockyer.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Outsiders get extra protection. For a third party dealing in good faith, the directors' power to bind the company is free of limits in the constitution.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And under the rule in Turquand's case, an outsider may assume the company's internal procedures were followed.
