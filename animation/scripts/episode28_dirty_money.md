## Scene 2: Three stages of laundering

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus H1(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Placement · layering · integration
- Nodes: Placement; Layering; Integration
- Arrows: p → l; l → i
- Caption: Criminal property: proceeds of ANY crime, incl. tax evasion

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Laundering usually has three stages.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Placement: dirty cash enters the financial system, for example as deposits, or a business's takings.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Layering: it moves through many transactions, accounts and countries, to hide where it came from.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Integration: it comes back looking clean, as profits, loans or property.
- **THE AUDITOR:** And remember: criminal property includes the proceeds of any crime. Theft, fraud, and tax evasion too.

## Scene 3: The offences

*Teaching board · Syllabus H1(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Money laundering offences
- Laundering — Conceal · arrange · acquire · up to 14 years
- Failure to disclose — Regulated sector: knowledge, suspicion or reasonable grounds
- Tipping off — Warning the suspect
- Caption: Defence: report to the MLRO

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In the UK model, the Proceeds of Crime Act creates three groups of offence.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Laundering itself: concealing, converting or transferring criminal property, arranging to help someone else do it, or acquiring, using or possessing it. Up to fourteen years in prison.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Failure to disclose: in the regulated sector, like accountancy, not reporting knowledge or suspicion of laundering, or where there are reasonable grounds to suspect it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And tipping off: telling a suspect that a report has been made, in a way likely to prejudice an investigation.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The key defence is reporting. Make a report to your firm's money laundering reporting officer, who decides whether to report to the authorities.

## Scene 4: Risk management and internal controls

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus H1(c)(i)–(ii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** (i) Risk management · (ii) Internal controls
- **Risk management:** Risk-based approach; Customer · country · product · transaction · channel; Written risk assessment; Grant charity low · street-cash charity high
- **Internal controls:** Appoint an MLRO; Senior manager responsible; Screen and train staff; Independent review

**Script**

- **THE AUDITOR:** Firms in the regulated sector must have policies, controls and procedures. The syllabus lists five areas.
- **THE AUDITOR:** First, risk management. Take a risk-based approach. Assess the firm's risks, by customer type, country, product, transaction and delivery channel, and write it down.
- **THE AUDITOR:** A small local charity funded by government grants is low risk. A charity collecting street cash and sending it abroad is much higher.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Second, internal controls. Appoint a money laundering reporting officer, give a senior manager responsibility, screen and train staff, and have the system independently checked.
- **NIA:** So every staff member should know who the MLRO is, and how to report.

## Scene 5: Customer due diligence

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus H1(c)(iii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** (iii) Customer due diligence
- **Simplified:** Clearly low risk; e.g. listed companies, regulated banks
- **Standard:** Identify and verify the customer; Beneficial owners (over 25%); Purpose of the relationship; Ongoing monitoring
- **Enhanced:** Politically exposed persons + family, associates; High-risk countries; Complex or unusually large deals
- Caption: Can't complete CDD? Don't act

**Script**

- **THE AUDITOR:** Third, customer due diligence, or know your customer.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Identify the customer, and verify it with reliable documents. Identify the beneficial owners, usually anyone owning or controlling more than twenty-five percent. Understand the purpose of the relationship. Then keep monitoring it.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Simplified due diligence may be allowed for clearly low-risk customers, such as listed companies or regulated banks.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Enhanced due diligence is required for high risk: politically exposed persons, and their families and close associates, high-risk countries, and complex or unusually large transactions.
- **THE AUDITOR:** And if you can't complete due diligence, you must not act for that customer.
- **KITO:** Our cash customer won't even give his full name. So, no deal.

## Scene 6: Records, reliance and compliance

*Teaching board · Syllabus H1(c)(iv)–(v)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** (iv) Records · (v) Monitoring compliance
- Reliance allowed, responsibility stays
- Keep records 5 years after the relationship
- Staff know the MLRO and procedures?
- Training proven and current?
- Random file test passes?
- Reporting patterns reviewed?

**Script**

- **THE AUDITOR:** Fourth, reliance and record keeping. A firm may rely on another regulated firm's due diligence, but it stays responsible if that turns out to be wrong.
- **THE AUDITOR:** And it must keep due diligence and transaction records, generally for five years after the relationship ends.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Fifth, monitoring and managing compliance, and communicating the policies internally. Ask yourself.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Would every staff member know who the MLRO is, and where the procedures are?
- **THE AUDITOR:** Can we prove everyone is trained, and kept up to date?
- **THE AUDITOR:** Would a random client file show a risk assessment and complete due diligence?
- **THE AUDITOR:** And do reporting patterns reveal teams that report unusually little?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A bought-in manual is not enough. Policies must fit the firm's actual risks.
