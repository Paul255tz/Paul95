## Scene 2: When risk passes `0:34`

*Route map (risk and costs) · Syllabus B2(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Passing of risk (Arts 67–69)
- **Art 67** Carriage involved: Risk passes to the first carrier
- **Art 67** Carrier at a named place: Risk passes at that place (e.g. the port)
- **Art 68** Goods sold in transit: Risk passes when the contract is made
- **Art 69** Other cases: When the buyer takes over the goods
- Caption: Goods must be clearly identified to the contract

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the contract involves carriage, and the seller need not hand the goods over at a particular place, risk passes when they are handed to the first carrier.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the seller must hand them to a carrier at a particular place, such as the port of Hamburg, risk passes there.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If goods are sold while already in transit, risk generally passes when the contract is made. Unless the seller already knew they were lost or damaged, and kept quiet.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In other cases, risk passes when the buyer takes over the goods. Or, if the buyer delays, when they are placed at its disposal.
- **NIA:** And keeping the documents, like the bill of lading, doesn't stop risk passing. But the goods must be clearly identified to the contract.

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** So if the parts were also the wrong specification, SafariTech could still avoid the contract, even though risk had passed.
- **KITO:** So for the soaked crates: risk passed to us in Hamburg. That's a claim on our insurance, not on Hans.

## Scene 4: After avoidance `1:56`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus B2(c)(v)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Effects of avoidance (Arts 81–84)
- Nodes: Contract avoided; Both released from obligations; Damages + dispute clause survive; Mutual restitution; Interest on refunds · account for benefits
- Arrows: av → rel; av → sur; av → res; av → int
- Caption: Can't return goods in substantially the same condition? Usually no avoidance

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** When a contract is avoided, both parties are released from their obligations.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But damages for the breach survive, and so does any dispute resolution clause, like an arbitration clause.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A party that has performed can claim back what it supplied or paid. Both sides make restitution at the same time.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A seller refunding the price must pay interest on it. A buyer returning goods must account for any benefit it got from them.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a buyer who cannot return the goods in substantially the condition received generally loses the right to avoid, or to demand substitutes.
- **NIA:** Unless that's not the buyer's fault, or the goods were used or sold normally before the defect was found.

## Scene 5: Preserving the goods `2:45`

*Side-by-side comparison · Port of Dar es Salaam · Syllabus B2(c)(vi)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Preservation of goods (Arts 85–88)
- **Seller preserves:** Buyer delays taking delivery; Or doesn't pay when due with delivery; May keep goods until costs repaid
- **Buyer preserves:** Received goods and means to reject; Goods at its disposal at destination (if no payment or undue hassle)
- **Either may:** Store with a third party, at the other's expense; Sell after unreasonable delay, with notice; MUST sell perishables; Keep costs, account for balance

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Whoever holds the goods must look after them, even when the other side is at fault.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the buyer delays taking delivery, or fails to pay when payment and delivery are due together, the seller must take reasonable steps to preserve the goods. It can keep them until its expenses are repaid.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the buyer has received the goods and intends to reject them, the buyer must preserve them. The same applies to goods put at its disposal at the destination, if it can take them without paying and without unreasonable inconvenience.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Either party may store the goods in a third party's warehouse, at the other side's expense, if the cost is reasonable.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the other side delays unreasonably, the goods may be sold, with reasonable notice. And perishable goods, or goods that are expensive to keep, must be sold. The seller keeps its reasonable costs, and accounts for the balance.
- **CAPTAIN CARGO:** So nobody gets to leave me rotting on the quay!
