## Scene 2: Promoters and their duties

*Teaching board · Syllabus D4(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Promoters
- Do the groundwork — Name · constitution · people · filings · costs
- Fiduciary duties — Skill and care · no secret profit
- Disclose interests — To an independent board or the members
- Remedies for breach — Rescind · recover secret profit · damages
- Caption: Erlanger v New Sombrero Phosphate (1878): rescission
- Caption: Gluckstein v Barnes (1900): account for secret profit

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A promoter is someone who undertakes to form a company, and set it going. Lawyers or accountants acting only in a professional capacity are not promoters.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Promoters do the groundwork: choose the name, draft the constitution, find directors and shareholders, file the documents, and pay the formation costs.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** They owe fiduciary duties to the company they are forming. They must act with reasonable skill and care, and not make secret profits.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Any profit or interest must be disclosed, to an independent board, or to the members.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Erlanger against New Sombrero Phosphate, promoters sold an island to their own company at a huge hidden profit. The company could rescind the sale.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If rescission is not possible, the company can recover the secret profit, as in Gluckstein against Barnes, or claim damages for breach of duty.

## Scene 3: Pre-incorporation contracts

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus D4(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Who is bound before incorporation?
- Nodes: Kito signs “for Safari Solar Ltd”; Landlord; Company not yet formed
- Arrows: k → l (lease); l → k (personally liable); c → l (can't ratify)
- Caption: Phonogram v Lane (1982): promoter liable
- Pop-up card **How to avoid personal liability**: Buy an off-the-shelf company · sign only a draft · novation after incorporation

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A company cannot ratify a contract made before it existed. Remember Kelner against Baxter.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And under the Companies Act, a contract made for a company not yet formed takes effect as a contract with the person who made it. That person is personally liable, unless the contract says otherwise.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Phonogram against Lane, a promoter signed for a company that was never formed, and had to pay personally.
- **KITO:** So I'm on the hook for the warehouse lease!
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Unless you avoid it. Buy a ready-made company off the shelf, or sign only a draft, to be signed by the company once it exists. Or after incorporation, the company can make a new contract with the landlord, replacing yours. That's novation.

## Scene 4: Registering the company

*Teaching board · Syllabus D4(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** The registration pack
- Memorandum of association — Subscribers form the company, take a share each
- Application for registration — Name · office · limited by shares/guarantee · private/public
- Capital and proposed officers — Statement of capital · directors (and secretary)
- Articles · significant control — Own articles, or model articles apply
- Statement of compliance + fee
- Pop-up card **Certificate of incorporation**: Conclusive evidence of incorporation (Jubilee Cotton Mills v Lewis). plc: trading certificate too.

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** To register, the promoters send the registrar an application with several documents.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The memorandum of association: the subscribers wish to form a company, and agree to become members, taking at least one share each.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The application itself: proposed name, where the registered office is, whether liability is limited by shares or guarantee, and whether the company is private or public.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A statement of capital and initial shareholdings, and a statement of the proposed officers.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Articles of association, unless the model articles will apply. And a statement of initial significant control.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A statement of compliance, and the fee.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If all is in order, the registrar issues a certificate of incorporation. It is conclusive evidence that the company exists from that date, as Jubilee Cotton Mills against Lewis shows. A public company also needs its trading certificate before it trades.

## Scene 6: Statutory books and returns

*Teaching board · Syllabus D4(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Statutory books
- Members
- Directors and secretaries — Including residential addresses
- Charges — Copies of instruments
- Minutes
- Accounting records
- People with significant control
- Pop-up card **Every year**: Annual accounts · confirmation statement (replaced the annual return)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Once formed, a company must keep statutory registers, usually at its registered office, open to inspection.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A register of members.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Registers of directors, and of their residential addresses, and of secretaries if any.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Copies of documents creating charges over its assets.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Minutes of meetings, and adequate accounting records.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a register of people with significant control.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Private companies may choose to keep some of this information on the central public register instead.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Each year, the company must also file accounts, and a confirmation statement, confirming the registrar's information is up to date. It replaced the old annual return.

## Scene 7: Who really controls it?

*Teaching board · Syllabus D4(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Person with significant control
- More than 25% of the shares
- More than 25% of the votes
- Can appoint or remove most directors
- Significant influence or control — Directly, or via a trust or firm
- Caption: Tanzania and Kenya: beneficial ownership filing
- Caption: UK: identity verification for directors and PSCs

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The register of people with significant control, the PSC register, shows who really stands behind a company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A PSC holds more than twenty-five percent of the shares,
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** or more than twenty-five percent of the votes,
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** or can appoint or remove a majority of the board,
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** or otherwise has the right to exercise, or actually exercises, significant influence or control, including through a trust or firm.
- **AMARA:** Banks check this to fight money laundering. And Tanzania and Kenya now require companies to file beneficial ownership information too.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And new UK reforms now require directors and PSCs to verify their identity with Companies House.
