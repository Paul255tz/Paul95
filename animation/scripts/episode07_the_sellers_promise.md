## Scene 2: Where is delivery? `0:33`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus B2(a)(i)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Place of delivery (Art 31)
- Nodes: No place agreed?; Carriage involved → hand to first carrier; Specific / known stock → at buyer's disposal there; Otherwise → seller's place of business
- Arrows: q → a; q → b; q → c
- Pop-up card **Arts 32: shipping duties**: Notify the consignment · appropriate carriage · suitable carrier for perishables
- Pop-up card **Arts 33–34: time and documents**: Fixed date · agreed period · or reasonable time. Documents as agreed.

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the contract names a place of delivery, deliver there. If not, the Convention fills the gap.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the contract involves carriage, the seller delivers by handing the goods to the first carrier.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the goods are specific, or come from a known stock or factory, the seller places them at the buyer's disposal at that place.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Otherwise, at the seller's own place of business.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If unidentified goods are shipped, the seller must notify the buyer which goods are his, and arrange appropriate carriage. Perishable goods need a suitable carrier.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Timing: on the date fixed, within any agreed period, or else within a reasonable time after the contract. And documents must be handed over at the time, place and in the form agreed.

## Scene 3: Conforming goods `1:26`

*Teaching board · Syllabus B2(a)(ii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Conformity (Arts 35–36)
- Fit for ordinary purposes
- Fit for a particular purpose made known
- Matches sample or model
- Usual or adequate packaging
- Pop-up card **Express terms first**: Quantity · quality · description · packaging as the contract requires
- Pop-up card **Timing of the defect**: Seller liable if the defect existed when risk passed, even if it appears later. Not if the buyer knew at the start.

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The goods must match the contract in quantity, quality and description, and be packaged as the contract requires.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Unless agreed otherwise, they must also meet four implied standards.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Fit for the purposes such goods are ordinarily used for.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Fit for any particular purpose the buyer made known, unless the buyer did not rely on the seller's skill.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Match any sample or model the seller showed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And be packed in the usual way, or adequately to protect them.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The seller is not liable if the buyer knew of the defect when contracting. But the seller is liable for any defect that existed when risk passed, even if it only shows up later.
- **NIA:** So machine five, the wrong model, fails the description test.

## Scene 4: Examine and notify `2:17`

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus B2(a)(ii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** The buyer's clock (Arts 38–40)
- Delivery
- Examine — As soon as practicable
- Notify seller — Reasonable time, specify the defect
- 2-year long stop — Unless a longer guarantee
- Caption: Seller knew and stayed silent? Can't rely on late notice

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Now the buyer's homework. Miss it, and the remedies vanish.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** On delivery, the buyer must examine the goods within as short a period as is practicable. If the goods are sent straight on, examination can wait until they reach the new destination.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Then give the seller notice of the defect, specifying its nature, within a reasonable time of discovering it, or when it ought to have been discovered.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And there is a long stop. No notice within two years of delivery means the right is lost, unless a longer guarantee applies.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** One exception: if the seller knew of the defect and kept quiet, the seller cannot rely on late notice.
- **KITO:** Nia, email Hans today, and describe exactly how machine two jams!

## Scene 5: Free from third-party claims `3:08`

*Side-by-side comparison · Bank (Nairobi) · Syllabus B2(a)(ii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Third-party claims (Arts 41–43)
- **Seller liable:** Goods free of third-party rights and claims; IP rights the seller knew or could not be unaware of; Under the law of the resale or use country, else the buyer's
- **Seller not liable:** Buyer agreed to take the goods subject to the claim; Buyer already knew of the IP right; Caused by the buyer's own designs or specifications

**Script**

- *SFX: whoosh*
- **AMARA:** One more check from the bank's side. Does anyone else claim these machines, or the technology inside them?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Good question. The seller must deliver goods free from any right or claim of a third party, unless the buyer agreed to take them subject to it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** For intellectual property, such as patents, trade marks and copyright, the seller is liable for rights it knew of, or could not have been unaware of.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** That means rights under the law of the country where the goods will be resold or used, if both parties contemplated it, or otherwise the buyer's own country.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But not if the buyer already knew of the right, or if the problem came from the buyer's own designs or specifications.
- **AMARA:** And the buyer must notify any claim within a reasonable time.

## Scene 6: The buyer's remedies `4:00`

*Teaching board · Syllabus B2(a)(iii)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Buyer's remedies (Arts 45–52)
- Require performance — Substitute only if fundamental · repair if reasonable
- Fix an additional period — Reasonable length
- Avoid the contract — Fundamental breach, by notice
- Reduce the price — In proportion to the defect
- Damages — On top of any other remedy
- Pop-up card **Fundamental breach (Art 25)**: Substantially deprives the other party of what it was entitled to expect. Seller may still cure (Art 48).

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the seller breaches, the buyer has a toolkit.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Require performance. Substitute goods only if the defect is a fundamental breach, and repair if that is reasonable.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Fix an additional period of reasonable length for the seller to perform.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Avoid the contract, by notice, for a fundamental breach, or for non-delivery within the additional period.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Reduce the price in proportion to the defect.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And claim damages, which can be added to any other remedy.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A breach is fundamental if it substantially deprives the other party of what it was entitled to expect. And the seller may still cure, without unreasonable delay or inconvenience, even after the delivery date.
- **NIA:** So for machine two, we ask for repair. For machine five, a replacement, if the wrong model is a fundamental breach.

## Scene 7: Too early, too much `4:58`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus B2(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Early and excess delivery (Arts 37, 52)
- **Early delivery:** Buyer may accept or refuse; Seller may cure a shortfall up to the date; Buyer keeps any damages claim
- **Excess quantity:** Refuse the excess; Or keep some or all of it; Pay for it at the contract rate

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Two more situations come up in exams.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the seller delivers before the date, the buyer may take delivery, or refuse it. And if goods are short, the seller may make up the shortfall up to the delivery date, if that causes no unreasonable inconvenience. The buyer keeps any right to damages.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If the seller delivers more than agreed, the buyer may refuse the excess, or keep some or all of it, paying for it at the contract rate.
- **KITO:** So if Hans sends six machines, I can keep the sixth, but I pay the contract price for it.
