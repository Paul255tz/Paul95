## Scene 2: Sole trader, partnership, company

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus D3(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Three ways to do business
- **Sole trader:** No formality; Full control; Unlimited liability
- **Partnership:** Shared work and profit; Little formality; Usually unlimited liability
- **Company:** Separate legal person; Perpetual succession; Limited liability; Can grant floating charges; Formality, publicity, regulation

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A sole trader is one person in business alone. No formality, full control, but unlimited personal liability.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A partnership shares the work and the profits. Still little formality, and, in an ordinary partnership, still unlimited liability.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A company is a separate legal person. It owns its property, makes its own contracts, and lives on when members change. Members usually have limited liability.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In exchange: formal registration, publicity, filing accounts, and more regulation.
- **NIA:** And a company can raise money more easily. It can give lenders a floating charge, which partners generally cannot.

## Scene 3: Corporations and limited liability

*Teaching board · Syllabus D3(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Corporations
- Corporation sole — One office holder, e.g. a mayor
- Corporation aggregate — Many members = one person
- Charter · statute · registration — Registration is by far the most common
- Pop-up card **Limited liability**: Members lose at most their unpaid share capital (or guarantee). The company pays its own debts.

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A corporation is an artificial legal person.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A corporation sole is a single office holder, like a mayor or a bishop. The office continues when the person changes.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A corporation aggregate is a group of people forming one legal person, like a company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Corporations can be created by royal charter, by a special statute, or, far more commonly, by registration under the Companies Act.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Limited liability means a member's liability is limited to any amount unpaid on their shares, or, in a guarantee company, the amount they guaranteed. The company itself is still fully liable for its own debts.
- **KITO:** So Safari Solar Limited could go bust, but our houses would be safe!

## Scene 4: Private and public companies

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus D3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Private vs public
- **Private (Ltd):** 1 director minimum; Secretary optional; No minimum capital; Cannot offer shares to the public; Kenya and Tanzania: max 50 members
- **Public (plc):** Limited by shares, named plc; £50,000 allotted · ¼ paid up; Trading certificate before trading; 2+ directors · qualified secretary; May offer shares to the public

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Companies may be limited by shares, limited by guarantee, or even unlimited. Most are private companies limited by shares.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company must be limited by shares, say it is public in its constitution, and end its name with p l c.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It needs allotted share capital of at least fifty thousand pounds, at least a quarter paid up, plus any premium. And it cannot trade until it receives a trading certificate.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It needs at least two directors, and a qualified company secretary. And only a public company may offer its shares to the public.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A private company needs just one director, no secretary, and no minimum capital.
- **NIA:** And in Kenya and Tanzania, a private company can have at most fifty members.

## Scene 5: Companies around the world

*Teaching board · Syllabus D3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Company forms abroad
- SA (France) — Like a public company
- SARL (France) — Like a private company
- AG (Germany) — Like a public company
- GmbH (Germany) — Like a private company
- SE (EU) — European public company · €120,000
- Islamic law — No separate corporate form

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Most countries have the same two-way split.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In France, the société anonyme, or S A, is like a public company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the S A R L is like a private company. Its capital is divided into parts that cannot be sold to the public.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Germany, the A G is like a public company, and the G m b H like a private one.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The European Company, the S E, is a public company for groups operating across the EU, with minimum capital of a hundred and twenty thousand euros.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And in Islamic law, there is no separate corporate form of its own, although partnership forms are recognised.
- **HANS:** My company is a G m b H. Now you know it's private!

## Scene 6: Salomon's case

*Case file · Courtroom · Syllabus D3(d)*

**On screen**

- **Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd** (House of Lords · 1897)
  - Facts: Sole trader incorporated, kept most shares and secured debentures
  - Claim: Liquidator: the company is just Salomon
  - Held: Separate legal person · Salomon paid as secured creditor
  - See also: Macaura (1925) · Lee v Lee's Air Farming (1961)
  - Stamp: SEPARATE PERSON

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The leading case is Salomon against Salomon and Company, decided by the House of Lords in 1897.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Mr Salomon sold his boot business to a company he formed. He held almost all the shares, and was also a secured creditor, through debentures.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** When the company failed, the liquidator argued the company was just Salomon in disguise.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The Lords disagreed. The company was a separate person, properly formed. Salomon, as a secured creditor, was paid first.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Other cases follow the same logic. In Macaura, a shareholder could not insure the company's timber, because it was not his. And in Lee against Lee's Air Farming, a director could also be his company's employee.

## Scene 7: Lifting the veil

*Teaching board · Syllabus D3(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Lifting the veil
- Sham to evade an obligation — Gilford Motor v Horne · Jones v Lipman
- Enemy character in wartime — Daimler v Continental Tyre
- Statute — Fraudulent / wrongful trading · no trading certificate · phoenix names · group accounts
- But rarely! — Adams v Cape · Prest v Petrodel

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Separate personality is a veil between a company and its members. Occasionally the courts look behind it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Where a company is a sham, used to evade a legal obligation. In Gilford Motor against Horne, a former employee set up a company to dodge his non-compete promise. And in Jones against Lipman, a seller transferred a house to his company to avoid selling it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In wartime, to find a company's enemy character, as in Daimler against Continental Tyre.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Under statute: fraudulent and wrongful trading, a plc trading without its certificate, reusing a failed company's name, and group accounts.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But courts are reluctant. In Adams against Cape Industries, they refused to treat a group as one entity. And Prest against Petrodel confirmed the veil is lifted only to stop evasion of an existing obligation.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Exam tip: Salomon is the rule. Lifting the veil is the exception.
