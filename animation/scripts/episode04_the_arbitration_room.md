## Scene 2: Court or arbitration? `0:36`

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus A3(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Court vs arbitration
- **Court:** Judges are legal experts; Precedent: predictable; Rights of appeal; Slow, formal, costly; Usually public
- **Arbitration:** Private and confidential; Expert arbitrator, chosen by the parties; Flexible, often quicker; Arbitrator may lack legal expertise; No precedent; Narrow grounds to challenge; Not always cheaper

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Court adjudication has real strengths.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Judges are legal experts. Decisions follow precedent, so outcomes are predictable, and there are rights of appeal.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But courts can be slow, formal and expensive, and hearings are usually public.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Arbitration is private. The parties choose an expert arbitrator, and a flexible procedure, which is often quicker and less stressful.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But an arbitrator may not be a legal expert, awards create no precedent, and the grounds to challenge an award are very narrow.
- **NIA:** And it isn't always cheaper. The parties pay the arbitrators and the venue.

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Arbitration is one of several ways to resolve a dispute outside court. Think of a spectrum.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In negotiation, the parties talk directly.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In mediation, a neutral mediator helps them reach their own agreement, but imposes nothing.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In conciliation, the neutral goes further, and proposes terms for settlement.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In arbitration, a tribunal makes a binding award.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And in litigation, a court gives a binding judgment.
- **NIA:** So moving along the line, the parties hand over more control.

## Scene 5: The UNCITRAL Model Law `2:39`

*Teaching board · Syllabus A3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Model Law: the key rules
- Agreement must be in writing — Signed document · letters or emails · reference to a clause
- Courts refer parties to arbitration — Unless the agreement is void or incapable of performance
- Tribunal rules on its own jurisdiction — The clause is separable from the main contract
- Interim measures — e.g. preserve goods or evidence
- Equal treatment, full opportunity — Each party can present its case
- Pop-up card **Local lens: East Africa**: Kenya: Arbitration Act 1995 (Model Law) · Tanzania: Arbitration Act 2020 (English-style)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Many countries base their arbitration law on the UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration. Kenya's Arbitration Act of 1995 follows it closely.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** First, the arbitration agreement must be in writing. A signed contract, an exchange of letters or emails, or a reference to a document containing the clause, will all do.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Second, a court must refer the parties to arbitration, unless the agreement is void or cannot be performed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Third, the tribunal can rule on its own jurisdiction. And the arbitration clause is treated as separate from the main contract, so it survives even if the contract is void.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Fourth, the tribunal may order interim measures, such as preserving goods or evidence.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And fifth, the parties must be treated equally, and each given a full opportunity to present its case.
- **NIA:** And Tanzania?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Tanzania's Arbitration Act of 2020 draws more on the English Arbitration Act, but shares the same core ideas.

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The parties can agree on the number of arbitrators. If they agree on one, they must also agree who it is, or a court or appointing authority will choose.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If they have not agreed the number, the Model Law says three.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Each party appoints one arbitrator.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And those two appoint the third, who presides.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Nationality alone cannot bar anyone. But every arbitrator must be impartial and independent, and must disclose anything that could raise justifiable doubts.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A party may challenge an arbitrator on those grounds, or for lacking the agreed qualifications. If an arbitrator cannot act, they withdraw, or the parties end the mandate, and a substitute is appointed.

## Scene 7: The award `4:48`

*Case file · Arbitration room · Syllabus A3(e)*

**On screen**

- **Final Award** (SafariTech Ltd v Hans GmbH · Seat: Nairobi)
  - Decision: By majority of the tribunal (or a consent award if they settle)
  - Form: In writing · signed · reasons · date and place
  - Fix-ups: Correction of clerical or calculation errors within 30 days
  - Effect: Binding and enforceable abroad · New York Convention 1958
  - Stamp: BINDING

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** With three arbitrators, decisions are made by a majority. If the parties settle, the tribunal can record it as a consent award.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An award must be in writing and signed. It must give reasons, unless the parties agree otherwise, and state its date and the place of arbitration.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Within thirty days, a party may ask the tribunal to correct typing or calculation errors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An award is binding, and can be enforced in any country, including through the New York Convention of 1958, which Tanzania and Kenya have both joined.
- **NIA:** So the award is final. Can it ever be cancelled?

## Scene 8: Setting an award aside `5:34`

*Teaching board · Syllabus A3(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Setting aside: the only grounds
- Incapacity or invalid agreement
- No proper notice / could not present case
- Outside the scope of the submission
- Tribunal or procedure not as agreed
- Not arbitrable · against public policy
- Caption: Court at the seat · within 3 months
- Caption: NO RE-HEARING OF THE MERITS

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Only rarely. The only route is an application to the court at the seat, made within three months of receiving the award.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The court does not re-hear the merits. A wrong decision on the facts or the law is not enough. The grounds are listed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A party lacked capacity, or the arbitration agreement was invalid.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A party was not given proper notice, or could not present its case.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The award deals with matters outside what was submitted.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The tribunal or procedure did not follow the agreement.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Or the court itself finds the subject cannot be arbitrated, or the award conflicts with public policy.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Almost the same grounds allow a court elsewhere to refuse recognition and enforcement.
