## Scene 2: The seven general duties

*Teaching board · Syllabus F1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** General duties (ss 171–177)
- Act within powers · proper purpose
- Promote the success of the company
- Independent judgement
- Reasonable care, skill and diligence
- Avoid conflicts of interest
- No benefits from third parties
- Declare interest in proposed deals

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** One. Act within your powers, under the constitution, and use powers only for their proper purpose.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Two. Promote the success of the company, for the benefit of its members as a whole.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Three. Exercise independent judgement.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Four. Exercise reasonable care, skill and diligence.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Five. Avoid conflicts of interest.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Six. Do not accept benefits from third parties because you are a director.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Seven. Declare any interest in a proposed transaction with the company.
- **NIA:** And Kenya's Companies Act of 2015 sets out the same duties, almost word for word.

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The long-term consequences of their decisions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The interests of employees.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Relationships with suppliers, customers and others.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The impact on the community and the environment.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A reputation for high standards of business conduct.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And acting fairly between members.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** When a company is insolvent, or close to it, directors must also consider the interests of its creditors.

## Scene 4: Care, skill and diligence

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Section 174: the dual test
- **Objective minimum:** What anyone in that role should know and do; Re D'Jan of London (1993)
- **Subjective top-up:** The director's own knowledge and skill; Experts held to a higher standard; Dorchester Finance v Stebbing (1989)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The standard of care has two parts.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An objective minimum: the knowledge, skill and experience reasonably expected of anyone doing that director's job.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Plus a subjective top-up: the director's own knowledge, skill and experience. So a qualified accountant like Nia is judged as an accountant.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Re D'Jan of London, a director signed an insurance form without reading it, and was negligent. And in Dorchester Finance against Stebbing, non-executives who signed blank cheques were liable too.
- **AMARA:** So non-executives can't just sit back. I must read every board paper.

## Scene 5: Conflicts: Kito's hotel deal

*Case file · Boardroom · Syllabus F1(d)*

**On screen**

- **Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver** (House of Lords · 1942)
  - Facts: Directors used an opportunity that came via the company
  - Held: Liable to account, even though honest
  - Also: IDC v Cooley (1972): diverted contract
  - Stamp: ACCOUNT FOR PROFIT

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Now Kito's hotel contract. A director must avoid situations where his interests conflict with the company's, especially exploiting property, information or opportunities.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Regal Hastings against Gulliver, directors bought shares personally, in an opportunity that came to them as directors. They acted honestly, and the company could not have afforded the shares anyway.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** They still had to hand over their profit to the company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In IDC against Cooley, a director secretly took a contract offered to him personally, but which he should have obtained for his company. He too had to account for his profit.
- **KITO:** Unless the independent directors authorise it. Fine, the hotel deal goes to Safari Solar.

## Scene 6: Proper purpose and remedies

*Teaching board · Syllabus F1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Breach of duty: remedies
- Account for profits
- Compensation for loss
- Rescission or injunction
- Ratification · court relief — Interested director can't vote · honest and reasonable
- Pop-up card **Howard Smith v Ampol (1974)**: Shares issued to defeat a takeover = improper purpose

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Powers must be used for proper purposes. In Howard Smith against Ampol Petroleum, directors issued shares to defeat a takeover. That was an improper purpose, so the issue was set aside.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Breach of duty has serious consequences.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Account for any profit made.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Compensate the company for its loss.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Rescission of the contract, or an injunction.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Members can ratify some breaches by ordinary resolution, without the votes of the director concerned. And a court may relieve a director who acted honestly and reasonably.

## Scene 7: Statutory controls

*Teaching board · Syllabus F1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Members' approval needed
- Service contracts over 2 years
- Substantial property transactions — > £100,000, or > 10% of net assets (min £5,000)
- Loans to directors — plc: also quasi-loans and credit transactions
- Payments for loss of office
- Caption: Exceptions: loans up to £10,000 · business expenses up to £50,000

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** On top of the general duties, some dealings need the members' approval.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A director's service contract guaranteed for more than two years.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A substantial property transaction. Buying or selling a non-cash asset with a director, worth over a hundred thousand pounds, or over ten percent of net assets, if that is above five thousand pounds. That's Kito's car!
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Loans to directors, and guarantees or security for them. Public companies need approval for quasi-loans and credit transactions too.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And payments for loss of office.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** There are exceptions, such as small loans up to ten thousand pounds, and up to fifty thousand to cover expenses of doing the company's business.
- **AMARA:** So Kito's car and loan both need a members' vote. And I'll be asking hard questions.
