## Scene 2: The purpose of administration

*Teaching board · Syllabus G1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Purpose: a strict hierarchy
- Rescue the company — As a going concern
- Better result for creditors — Than an immediate winding up
- Realise property — For secured or preferential creditors

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An administrator must pursue one objective, in a strict order.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** First: rescue the company as a going concern.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If that's not reasonably practicable: achieve a better result for the creditors as a whole than an immediate winding up would.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And only if neither is practicable: realise property to pay the secured or preferential creditors, without unnecessarily harming the creditors as a whole.
- **NIA:** So the company itself comes first. Selling it off is the last resort.

## Scene 3: Three ways to appoint

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus G1(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Appointing an administrator
- Nodes: Administrator (insolvency practitioner); Court order; Floating charge holder; Company or directors
- Arrows: court → adm (insolvent + likely to work); qfch → adm (out of court); dirs → adm (notice to charge holder)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An administrator can be appointed in three ways.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** By court order, on application by the company, its directors, or creditors. The court must be satisfied the company is, or is likely to become, unable to pay its debts, and that administration is reasonably likely to achieve its purpose.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Out of court, by a qualifying floating charge holder, a lender with a floating charge over the whole, or substantially the whole, of the company's property. Like Amara's bank.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Or out of court, by the company or its directors. They must give the floating charge holder notice, so it can appoint its own choice instead.
- **AMARA:** Which is why banks like that floating charge. It gives us control of who runs the rescue.

## Scene 4: The moratorium

*Teaching board · Syllabus G1(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Effects of appointment
- No winding up — Petitions dismissed or suspended
- No enforcing security or repossessing
- Legal proceedings frozen
- Administrative receiver leaves
- Directors need the administrator's consent

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Appointment brings a moratorium, a legal shield around the company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** No winding-up order or resolution can be made, and pending petitions are dismissed or suspended.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** No security can be enforced, and no goods repossessed, without the administrator's consent or the court's permission.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Legal proceedings against the company are frozen on the same basis.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Any administrative receiver must leave office.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the directors stay in office, but can only use their management powers with the administrator's consent.
- **KITO:** So I can't petition, or sue Kilimanjaro, while the administrator works. I'll wait.

## Scene 5: The administrator at work

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus G1(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Administration timeline
- Appointed — Officer of the court · company's agent
- Notify + statement — Creditors, registrar · directors' statement
- Proposals — Within 8 weeks · creditors decide
- Manage — Sell, appoint, pay, challenge
- Ends — After 1 year unless extended

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The administrator is an officer of the court, and acts as the company's agent. They must act in the interests of the creditors as a whole, as quickly and efficiently as reasonably practicable.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** First, notify the company, the creditors and the registrar, and get a statement of affairs from the directors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Within eight weeks, send proposals to the creditors, who decide whether to approve them.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Then manage the business in line with the proposals. The administrator can sell assets, appoint or remove directors, pay creditors, and challenge earlier transactions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Administration ends automatically after one year, unless extended by the creditors or the court. Or earlier, when its purpose is achieved, often moving into a liquidation or a creditors' arrangement.
- **AMARA:** And Kilimanjaro's shops kept trading the whole time.

## Scene 6: Why choose administration?

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus G1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Administration vs liquidation
- **Administration:** Company may survive; Moratorium: time to plan; Often better returns; Jobs can be saved
- **Liquidation:** Final; Business broken up; Staff dismissed; Company dissolved

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Compared with liquidation, administration has real advantages.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The company may survive. Creditors are held back, giving time to plan. Creditors often recover more, and keep a customer. Members may keep shares in a viable company. And jobs can be saved.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Liquidation is final. The company is broken up, its staff dismissed, and it is dissolved.
- **NIA:** Kenya's Insolvency Act of 2015 introduced administration too, for the same reasons.
