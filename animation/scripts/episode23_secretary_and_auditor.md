## Scene 2: Does a company need a secretary?

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F2(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** The company secretary
- **Private company:** Optional (UK); Unless the articles require one; Tanzania: every company · Kenya: larger companies
- **Public company:** Must have one; Appointed by the directors; Qualified: ACCA, lawyer, 3 of 5 years, or capable

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In the UK, a private company does not need a secretary, unless its articles require one. The directors then handle the paperwork.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company must have one, appointed by the directors, who must make sure the secretary is suitably qualified.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** For example, a member of a recognised body like ACCA, a lawyer, someone who was a public company secretary for three of the last five years, or someone the directors judge capable.
- **NIA:** East Africa is stricter. In Tanzania, every company must have a secretary, and in Kenya, larger private companies must too.

## Scene 3: What the secretary does

*Teaching board · Syllabus F2(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Secretary's role
- Statutory registers and records
- Filings with the registrar
- Meetings, notices, minutes
- Governance advice · NED induction
- Pop-up card **Panorama v Fidelis (1971)**: Secretary has apparent authority for administrative contracts, like hiring cars

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The directors decide the secretary's duties, but they are mainly administrative.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Keeping the statutory registers and records.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Filing documents with the registrar, like the confirmation statement, accounts and charges.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Organising meetings, sending notices, and taking minutes.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And advising the board on governance and compliance, including inducting new non-executive directors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A secretary has apparent authority for administrative contracts. In Panorama Developments against Fidelis Furnishing Fabrics, a secretary hired cars, claiming they were for company business, then used them himself. The company still had to pay.
- **THE AUDITOR:** But not authority for big commercial deals, like buying a factory.

## Scene 4: Appointing an auditor

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus F2(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Appointing the auditor
- Audit required — Unless exempt (small, dormant)
- Directors — First auditor · casual vacancy
- Members — Ordinary resolution · private often deemed reappointed
- Secretary of State — If no one else does
- Caption: Eligible: recognised body member + independent

**Script**

- **THE AUDITOR:** Every company must have its accounts audited, unless it is exempt, for example as a small or dormant company.
- **THE AUDITOR:** The directors appoint the first auditor, and fill casual vacancies.
- **THE AUDITOR:** After that, the members appoint by ordinary resolution. For a public company, that is at the meeting where the accounts are laid. A private company's auditor is often deemed reappointed.
- **THE AUDITOR:** If no one appoints an auditor, the Secretary of State can.
- **THE AUDITOR:** To be eligible, I must belong to a recognised supervisory body, like ACCA, and be independent. No officer or employee of the company, or their partner or employee, may audit it.

## Scene 5: Rights and duties

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F2(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Auditor's rights and duties
- **Rights:** Access to books at all times; Information and explanations; Notice of, attend and speak at meetings; Copies of written resolutions
- **Duties:** Report: true and fair view?; Directors' report consistent?; Quoted: remuneration report; By exception: records, information

**Script**

- **THE AUDITOR:** My rights let me do the job.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Access to the company's books and records at all times. Information and explanations from officers and staff. Misleading me is a crime.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Notice of, and attendance at, general meetings, and the right to be heard. And copies of proposed written resolutions.
- **THE AUDITOR:** My main duty is to report to the members on whether the accounts give a true and fair view, and are properly prepared.
- **THE AUDITOR:** I also report whether the directors' report is consistent with the accounts, and for quoted companies, on parts of the remuneration report.
- **THE AUDITOR:** And I report by exception if proper accounting records were not kept, or I didn't get the information I needed.
- **KITO:** So you're not checking every lamp, just whether the accounts are true and fair.

## Scene 6: Who can sue the auditor?

*Case file · Courtroom · Syllabus F2(b)*

**On screen**

- **Caparo Industries plc v Dickman** (House of Lords · 1990)
  - Facts: Investor relied on audited accounts to buy shares
  - Held: Duty owed to the company and members as a body only
  - Stamp: NO DUTY TO INVESTOR

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An auditor's report is relied on by many people. But who can sue if it's negligent?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In Caparo Industries against Dickman, an investor bought shares relying on audited accounts, then sued the auditors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The House of Lords held the auditors owed their duty to the company, and to the members as a body, to help them oversee the directors. Not to individual investors making investment decisions.
- **THE AUDITOR:** Which is why I write my report to the members.

## Scene 7: Removal and resignation

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F2(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** When the auditor leaves
- **Removal:** Ordinary resolution + special notice; At a meeting, never written; Written reps · right to speak; Registrar told in 14 days
- **Resignation:** Written notice to registered office; Statement of circumstances; May requisition a meeting

**Script**

- **THE AUDITOR:** Members can remove me at any time, by ordinary resolution with special notice, at a meeting. Not by written resolution.
- **THE AUDITOR:** I may send written representations to the members, and speak at the meeting. The company tells the registrar within fourteen days.
- **THE AUDITOR:** If I resign, I give written notice to the registered office.
- **THE AUDITOR:** And I must deposit a statement of the circumstances connected with my leaving, so creditors and members can see if something is wrong. I may even require the directors to call a meeting, to explain.
- **NIA:** So an auditor can't be quietly pushed out, or quietly walk away.
