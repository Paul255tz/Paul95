## Scene 2: AGMs and general meetings

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F3(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Meetings of members
- **Public company:** AGM required; Within 6 months of year end; Accounts · directors · auditor · dividend
- **Private company:** No AGM needed; Unless the articles require one; Written resolutions instead
- Caption: General meeting = any meeting of members

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A general meeting is any meeting of the company's members.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company must also hold an annual general meeting, within six months after its financial year end.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Typical AGM business: laying the accounts and reports, electing directors, appointing the auditor, and declaring a dividend.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A private company does not need an AGM, unless its articles require one. It can take most decisions by written resolution instead.
- **HANS:** So I may never get a meeting, but I still get a vote on the resolutions.

## Scene 3: Who can call a meeting?

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus F3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Calling a general meeting
- Nodes: General meeting; Directors; Members with 5%+; The court; Resigning auditor
- Arrows: d → gm (usually); m → d (require); m → gm (if directors don't); c → gm; a → gm
- Caption: Call within 21 days · hold within 28 days of notice

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Normally, the directors call general meetings.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But members holding at least five percent of the paid-up voting capital can require them to. The directors must call it within twenty-one days, for a date within twenty-eight days of the notice.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If they don't, those members can call it themselves, at the company's expense.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A court can order a meeting where it's impractical to call one, for example to break a deadlock.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a resigning auditor can require one too.
- **HANS:** Ten percent! So I could force a meeting if I needed to.

## Scene 4: Notice periods

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus F3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** How much notice?
- 14 clear days — General meeting
- 21 days — Public company AGM
- Short notice — 90% private · 95% public · plc AGM 100%
- Special notice — 28 days: remove director/auditor
- Caption: Notice: when, where, business, special resolution text, proxies

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Members must get proper notice.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** At least fourteen clear days for a general meeting.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Twenty-one days for a public company's AGM.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Short notice is possible, if enough members agree: ninety percent in a private company, ninety-five in a public one. For a public company's AGM, every member must agree.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And special notice, twenty-eight days to the company, for resolutions to remove a director or an auditor.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The notice states the date, time, place, and general nature of the business, the full text of any special resolution, and members' rights to appoint proxies.

## Scene 5: Three kinds of resolution

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F3(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Resolutions
- **Ordinary:** Over 50% of votes cast; The default; e.g. appoint or remove a director
- **Special:** At least 75%; Articles · name · capital · voluntary winding up; Text in notice · file in 15 days
- **Written:** Private companies only; Majority of ALL eligible votes; Not to remove director or auditor; Copy to the auditor

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An ordinary resolution needs a simple majority, more than fifty percent of the votes cast. It is the default, for example to appoint or remove a director.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A special resolution needs at least seventy-five percent. It is needed to change the articles, change the name, reduce capital, or wind up voluntarily. Its exact text must be in the notice, and a copy filed within fifteen days.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A written resolution is for private companies only. It is circulated, and passes when enough of all eligible members sign: a majority for ordinary, seventy-five percent for special.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** But a written resolution cannot remove a director or an auditor, and the auditor must receive a copy.
- **NIA:** So a written resolution counts all the members, not just the ones who turn up.

## Scene 6: Quorum and proxies

*Teaching board · Syllabus F3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Quorum and proxies
- Quorum: 2 (or 1 for a sole member)
- No quorum? Adjourn
- Proxy: attend, speak, vote, demand a poll
- Forms: no earlier than 48 hours before

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A meeting needs a quorum, the minimum number present for valid business. Unless the articles say otherwise, it is two qualifying persons, or one for a single-member company.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If there's no quorum, the meeting is adjourned, as the articles provide.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Every member may appoint a proxy, who need not be a member, to attend, speak and vote for them, on a show of hands and on a poll. A proxy can also demand a poll.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The company cannot require proxy forms more than forty-eight hours before the meeting. And a corporate member appoints a representative.
- **HANS:** Perfect. When I'm in Hamburg, Nia can be my proxy!

## Scene 7: Hands up, or count the shares?

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus F3(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Show of hands vs poll
- **Show of hands:** One vote per person present; Shareholding ignored
- **Poll:** Votes counted by shares; Demand: 5+ members, 10% votes, or 10% capital; Or the chair; Abstentions don't count

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Voting starts with a show of hands: one vote per person present, whatever their shareholding.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A poll counts votes by shares, usually one vote per share. So big shareholders often demand one.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The articles cannot stop a poll being demanded by five or more members, or by members holding ten percent of the voting rights, or ten percent of the paid-up capital. The chair can also call one.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And abstentions count neither for nor against.
- **KITO:** On a show of hands Hans equals me. On a poll, I win easily!
