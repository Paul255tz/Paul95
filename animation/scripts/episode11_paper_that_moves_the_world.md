## Scene 2: Three jobs in one document `0:30`

*Teaching board · Syllabus C1(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** A bill of lading is…
- A receipt for the goods — Quantity, apparent condition, on board
- Evidence of the contract of carriage — Between shipper and carrier
- A document of title — Transfer the bill → transfer the right to the goods
- Caption: Shows when goods reached the carrier → often when risk passes
- Caption: Clean = no defects noted · Claused = damage recorded

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A bill of lading is issued by the carrier, or its agent, to the shipper. It does three jobs.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It is a receipt, confirming the carrier has received the goods, in the quantity and apparent condition stated, and usually that they are on board a named vessel.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It is evidence of the contract of carriage between shipper and carrier.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And it is a document of title. Transferring a negotiable bill transfers the right to the goods.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It also shows when the goods were handed to the carrier, which often marks when risk passes, unless the contract says otherwise.
- **NIA:** And a clean bill means no defects were noted. A claused bill records damage, and banks don't like those.

## Scene 3: Kinds of bill `1:18`

*Teaching board · Syllabus C1(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Types of bill of lading
- Negotiable — To order · endorse and transfer
- Non-negotiable — Straight · named consignee only
- Inland — Overland to the port
- Ocean — Port to port
- Through — Inland + ocean combined
- Air waybill — Always non-negotiable

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Bills come in several kinds.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A negotiable bill is made out to order. The holder endorses and hands it on, and the right to the goods goes with it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A non-negotiable, or straight, bill names the consignee. The carrier delivers only to that person.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An inland bill covers carriage over land to the port of departure.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An ocean bill covers the sea voyage, from port to port.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A through bill combines both, from the factory all the way to the foreign port.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And an air waybill covers carriage by air. It is always non-negotiable, so it is not a document of title.
- **CAPTAIN CARGO:** For Dubai, Kito chose a through bill, from Arusha by rail, then by sea with me!

## Scene 4: What is a bill of exchange? `2:06`

*Teaching board · Syllabus C1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Bill of exchange: the elements
- Unconditional order in writing
- Drawer → addressed to the drawee
- Signed by the drawer
- Sum certain in money
- On demand, or a fixed/determinable date
- To a payee, to order, or to bearer
- Pop-up card **Promissory note**: A promise by the maker to pay, not an order to someone else

**Script**

- **AMARA:** Now, how does the money move? Often with a bill of exchange.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A bill of exchange is an unconditional order in writing, addressed by one person, the drawer, to another, the drawee.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It is signed by the drawer, and requires the drawee to pay a sum certain in money, on demand, or at a fixed or determinable future time.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Payment goes to, or to the order of, a specified person, the payee, or to the bearer.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A promissory note is similar, but it is a promise by the maker to pay, rather than an order to someone else.
- **KITO:** So the bill says: Dubai buyer, pay SafariTech fifty thousand dollars in ninety days.

## Scene 5: When is a bill international? `2:50`

*Teaching board · Syllabus C1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** UN Convention on International Bills (1988)
- Headed: “International bill of exchange (UNCITRAL Convention)”
- Names at least 2 of the 5 places — Drawn · drawer · drawee · payee · payment
- Two of them in different states
- Caption: Not yet in force: 5 of 10 required states

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** UNCITRAL's Convention on International Bills of Exchange and International Promissory Notes, of 1988, offers an optional global regime.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The instrument must be headed, and say in its text, International bill of exchange, UNCITRAL Convention.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It must name at least two of five places: where it was drawn, next to the drawer's signature, next to the drawee's name, next to the payee's name, and the place of payment.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And at least two of those places must be in different states.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A reality check: it needs ten states to come into force, and so far only five have joined. In practice, national laws on bills still govern. But the exam tests the Convention's rules.

## Scene 6: The life of a bill `3:41`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus C1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Parties to a bill
- Nodes: Drawer (SafariTech); Drawee → Acceptor; Payee → Indorser; Indorsee / Holder; Guarantor
- Arrows: dr → de (draws on); de → pa (accepted); pa → in (endorses); gu → de (guarantees)
- Caption: Endorsed in blank → bearer bill, passes by delivery

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Follow the bill. SafariTech, as drawer, draws it on the Dubai buyer's bank, the drawee.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The drawee signs it as accepted, and becomes the acceptor, primarily liable to pay.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The bill goes to the payee. If the payee wants cash now, it can endorse the bill to its own creditor, the indorsee, who can endorse it again.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** An endorsement can name the next holder, or be signed in blank. A bill endorsed in blank becomes a bearer bill, and passes by simple delivery.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a guarantor can sign to guarantee payment, usually by the acceptor.
- **AMARA:** That's how our bank can buy the bill today at a discount, and collect in full in ninety days.

## Scene 7: Dishonour `4:28`

*Animated diagram · Studio · Syllabus C1(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Dishonour: who pays?
- Nodes: Acceptor refuses; Holder; Drawer; Indorsers; Guarantor
- Arrows: ho → ac (dishonour); ho → d1; ho → d2; ho → d3
- Caption: Jointly and severally liable
- Caption: Protected holder: free from most defences

**Script**

- **KITO:** And if the acceptor refuses to pay?
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Then the bill is dishonoured. The holder can claim against everyone who signed before it: the drawer, every indorser, and any guarantor.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** They are jointly and severally liable, so the holder can pick any of them. Whoever pays can then claim back up the chain.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Under the Convention, a protected holder, who took the bill complete, in good faith, and without notice of any problem, takes it free from most defences.
- **NIA:** So every signature on the bill is like another promise to pay.
