## Scene 2: Three routes to liquidation

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus G1(a)–(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Types of liquidation
- **Members' voluntary:** Solvent company; Members decide; Creditors paid in full
- **Creditors' voluntary:** Insolvent company; Members start it; Creditors take control
- **Compulsory:** Court order; Usually a creditor's petition

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A members' voluntary liquidation is for a solvent company. The members decide to close it down, and every creditor will be paid in full.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A creditors' voluntary liquidation is for an insolvent company. The members start it, but the creditors take control.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And a compulsory liquidation is ordered by the court, usually on a creditor's petition.
- **NIA:** So the key question for a voluntary liquidation is: is the company solvent?

## Scene 3: Voluntary liquidation step by step

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus G1(a)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** MVL vs CVL
- **MVL (solvent):** Directors' declaration of solvency; Debts paid within 12 months; Made within 5 weeks before the resolution; No reasonable grounds = crime; Special resolution · members appoint liquidator
- **CVL (insolvent):** No declaration; Special resolution; Creditors decide within 14 days; Statement of affairs; Creditors' liquidator prevails

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In a members' voluntary liquidation, the directors first make a declaration of solvency.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** After full inquiry, they declare the company will pay its debts in full, with interest, within a stated period of no more than twelve months. It must be made within the five weeks before the resolution.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Making it without reasonable grounds is a crime.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The members then pass a special resolution to wind up, and appoint a liquidator. If the liquidator finds the company is insolvent after all, it becomes a creditors' liquidation.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** In a creditors' voluntary liquidation, there's no declaration. The members pass a special resolution, and within fourteen days the creditors decide, usually by a virtual meeting or deemed consent, who the liquidator will be. The directors provide a statement of affairs.
- **NIA:** And if members and creditors nominate different liquidators, the creditors' choice wins.

## Scene 4: Grounds for compulsory liquidation

*Teaching board · Syllabus G1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Compulsory winding up: grounds
- Special resolution for court winding up
- plc: no trading certificate after 1 year
- No business started, or suspended, for a year
- Unable to pay debts — e.g. statutory demand over £750 unpaid for 3 weeks
- Just and equitable — Yenidje (deadlock) · Ebrahimi (quasi-partnership)

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A court may order winding up on several grounds. The most important are these.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The company has resolved, by special resolution, to be wound up by the court.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company has had no trading certificate for over a year.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It hasn't started business within a year, or has suspended it for a whole year.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It is unable to pay its debts. For example, a creditor owed more than seven hundred and fifty pounds serves a statutory demand, which is unpaid after three weeks.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Or it is just and equitable. Deadlock, in Re Yenidje Tobacco. A member shut out of a quasi-partnership, in Ebrahimi against Westbourne Galleries. Or the company's main purpose has failed.
- **KITO:** Kilimanjaro owes us forty thousand dollars, undisputed. We serve a statutory demand, and if it's unpaid in three weeks, we petition!

## Scene 5: After the winding-up order

*Teaching board · Syllabus G1(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Effects of a winding-up order
- Official receiver becomes liquidator
- Starts from the petition — Later dispositions void unless court approves
- Legal actions halted
- Directors' powers end · staff dismissed
- Challenge past transactions — Undervalue · preference · late floating charge

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A petition can be presented by the company, its directors, creditors, contributories, or the government.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Once the court orders winding up, the official receiver becomes liquidator, until an insolvency practitioner is appointed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The liquidation is treated as starting when the petition was presented, so later dispositions of property are void unless the court approves them.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Legal actions against the company are halted.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The directors' powers end, and employees are dismissed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the liquidator can challenge earlier deals: transactions at an undervalue, preferences to favoured creditors, and late floating charges, and pursue directors for fraudulent or wrongful trading.
- **NIA:** So the liquidator steps into the directors' shoes, and looks backwards too.

5. Prescribed part (for unsecured)

- Animated: money pours down the tiers in order of priority

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Now the order of payment. Learn it carefully.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** One. Fixed charge holders, from the assets they hold charges over.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Two. The costs and expenses of the liquidation.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Three. Preferential creditors: employees' unpaid wages, up to four months and eight hundred pounds each, plus holiday pay and pension contributions.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Four. Secondary preferential creditors: the tax authority, for taxes like VAT and PAYE that the company collected for it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Five. The prescribed part: a slice of floating charge assets ring-fenced for unsecured creditors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Six. Floating charge holders.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Seven. Unsecured creditors, including any excess wages.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Eight. Deferred debts, such as dividends declared but unpaid.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And nine. Members, preference shareholders first, if their terms say so.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Watch the money pour down. Each class is paid in full before the next gets anything. If there's not enough, that class shares rateably.

## Scene 7: The prescribed part

*Character scene · Studio · Syllabus G1(c)*

**On screen**

- Pop-up card **Prescribed part**: 50% of the first £10,000 + 20% of the rest · cap £800,000
- Pop-up card **Example: £100,000 of floating charge assets**: £5,000 + 20% × £90,000 (£18,000) = £23,000 for unsecured

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The prescribed part protects unsecured creditors from floating charge holders taking everything.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It is fifty percent of the first ten thousand pounds of floating charge assets, plus twenty percent of the rest, up to a maximum of eight hundred thousand pounds.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** So if floating charge assets are a hundred thousand pounds, the prescribed part is five thousand, plus eighteen thousand. Twenty-three thousand pounds for the unsecured creditors.
- **KITO:** So as an unsecured creditor of Kilimanjaro, we'd share in that. Better than nothing!
