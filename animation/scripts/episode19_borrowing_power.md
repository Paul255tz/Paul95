## Scene 2: Borrowing powers and debentures

*Teaching board · Syllabus E2(a)–(b)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Debentures
- Single debenture — One lender, e.g. a bank
- Series of debentures — Many lenders, rank pari passu
- Debenture stock — Held via a trustee and trust deed
- Pop-up card **Power to borrow**: Implied for trading companies · exercised by directors · plc needs its trading certificate first

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A trading company has an implied power to borrow for its business. And with unrestricted objects, a modern company can borrow unless its articles restrict it. The directors exercise the power.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A public company cannot borrow until it has its trading certificate.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Loan capital is money borrowed long term. The document acknowledging the debt is called a debenture.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It can be a single debenture, to one lender like a bank.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A series of debentures, issued to many lenders, ranking equally, pari passu.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Or debenture stock, held through a trustee under a trust deed for the benefit of all the holders.
- **AMARA:** And it can be secured by a charge over assets, or unsecured.

## Scene 3: Shares or loans?

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus E2(c)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Share capital vs loan capital
- **Shareholder:** A member: owns the company; Dividend only from distributable profits; Usually votes; Repaid last on winding up; No discount · buy-backs controlled
- **Debenture holder:** A creditor: owed money; Interest due, profits or not; Usually no vote; Repaid before members; can enforce security; May issue at discount · buy back freely

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Debenture holders and shareholders are very different.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Shareholders are members, who own the company. Debenture holders are creditors, who are owed money by it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Interest on debentures must be paid, even with no profits. Dividends can only come from distributable profits.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Debenture holders usually have no vote. On a winding up, they are repaid before any shareholder, and secured ones can enforce their security.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And debentures may be issued at a discount, and bought back freely. Shares may not be issued at a discount, and buy-backs are tightly controlled.
- **NIA:** So shares are riskier for the investor, but debt is riskier for the company.

## Scene 4: Fixed and floating charges

*Side-by-side comparison · Studio · Syllabus E2(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Fixed vs floating
- **Fixed charge:** Specific, identified assets; Land, buildings, machinery; Can't sell without consent; Ranks ahead of floating charges
- **Floating charge:** A class of assets, present and future; Changes in ordinary business; Company deals freely until crystallisation; Stock, receivables
- Caption: Re Spectrum Plus (2005): substance beats the label

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A charge gives a lender rights over company assets if the loan is not repaid.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A fixed charge attaches to specific, identified assets, like land, buildings or major machinery. The company cannot sell or deal with them without the lender's consent.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A floating charge, as described in Re Yorkshire Woolcombers, is a charge over a class of assets, present and future, that changes in the ordinary course of business.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Until it crystallises, the company can deal with those assets freely. Think inventory and receivables.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The label the parties use doesn't decide it. In Re Spectrum Plus, a so-called fixed charge over book debts was really floating, because the company could freely use the money it collected.
- **AMARA:** Which is why banks prefer fixed charges wherever they can.

## Scene 5: Crystallisation and weak spots

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus E2(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** When a floating charge crystallises
- Liquidation
- Stops trading
- Receiver or administrator
- Event named in the charge
- Pop-up card **Weak spots**: Behind fixed charges, expenses and preferential creditors · prescribed part for unsecured
- Pop-up card **Timing and negative pledges**: Created within 12 months (2 years connected) may be invalid, except new money · negative pledge binds if known

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A floating charge crystallises, becoming fixed on the assets held at that moment, when certain events happen.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The company goes into liquidation.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** It stops trading.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A receiver or administrator is appointed.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Or an event named in the charge itself occurs.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Floating charges have weak spots. They rank behind fixed charges, liquidation expenses and preferential creditors, and part of their assets is set aside for unsecured creditors.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A floating charge created in the twelve months before insolvency, or two years for a connected person, may be invalid, except to the extent of new money given. And a negative pledge clause can stop the company giving later charges priority, if the later lender knows of it.

## Scene 6: Registering charges

*Timeline · Studio · Syllabus E2(e)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Registration of charges
- Charge created — Clock starts next day
- 21 days to register
- Certificate — Conclusive evidence
- Missed it? — Void vs liquidator, administrator, creditors
- Caption: Court may allow late registration

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Most charges must be registered at Companies House within twenty-one days, beginning the day after the charge is created.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** The registrar issues a certificate, which is conclusive evidence that the charge was properly registered.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** If it isn't registered in time, the charge is void against a liquidator, an administrator and other creditors. The debt survives, but it becomes unsecured, and immediately repayable.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A court can allow late registration, without harming rights already acquired. And the company keeps copies of its charge documents for inspection.
- **AMARA:** Our lawyers register every charge on day one. In Tanzania and Kenya too, charges must be registered with the companies registry, within a set time.

## Scene 7: Who ranks first?

*Teaching board · Syllabus E2(d)*

**On screen**

**Heading:** Priority of charges
- Fixed beats floating
- Unless a negative pledge is known
- Same type: first created, if registered
- Or by priority agreement

**Script**

- **PROFESSOR LEX:** When two charges cover the same asset, the rules on priority decide.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** A fixed charge ranks ahead of a floating charge, even one created earlier.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Unless the floating charge has a negative pledge clause, and the later fixed chargee knew about it.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** Between charges of the same type, the earlier one created ranks first, provided each was registered on time.
- **PROFESSOR LEX:** And the parties can always change the order by a priority agreement.
- **KITO:** So the bank's fixed charge on the factory beats everyone else on that building.
